# wikiPhidia

Flashcards for learning Chinese and Japanese, with spaced repetition, YouTube clips, dictionaries and a vocabulary deck built from your notes.

The whole app is one file, `index.html`. There is no build step and nothing to install: open it in a browser, or host it anywhere that serves static files. On a phone it can be added to the home screen and runs full screen.

## Lessons and cards

- Paste cards into a lesson, one per line, as `question : answer` or tab-separated. The separator is detected and a preview shows what will be imported. Column delimiter offers Auto-detect, Tab and Colon ( : ), in Lessons and Vocabulary alike; only Tab also takes a time column and a notes column. **Auto-detect**, the first choice and the default on both sides, reads a whole study text in one paste, in Lessons or in Vocabulary alike: a question line just above a paragraph (ending in ?, starting with Q: or Question:, or a short heading such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes one card with the whole paragraph as its answer (an A: or Answer: in front is dropped; following paragraphs belong to the same answer), any other paragraph becomes one question-only card per sentence, each `word = meaning` or `word: meaning` line (with or without a `*`, `-`, `•` or `1.` in front) becomes a word, and a short line just above a word list (such as `🔥 Phrasal verbs to conquer today`) is left out. Each word is filed under the card whose question or answer uses it, also in another form (`headed out` for head out, `got up` for get up, `gave up` for give up), so clicking it there shows that sentence, and the back of its flashcard shows the sentence with that form highlighted (the back always uses a sentence of the lesson that contains the word, even if the word was filed under another card); a word no sentence uses goes to the lesson's own word list. A short first line without sentence punctuation (such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes the lesson title when the lesson is empty, and stays the question of its card when a paragraph follows it. On the Vocabulary side, other short lines become words without a meaning, which can be filled in later. From Auto-detect, pasted cards or words still switch to Tab or colon by themselves when more than half the lines use it and no line holds several sentences (a colon after a finished sentence does not count, nor do Q:, A:, Question: and Answer: labels, so a paste of short Q: and A: lines stays questions and answers); from any other choice, Auto-detect is picked when no line has a Tab or colon. When a paste into a text lesson (from Lessons or Vocabulary) has no word list, its sentences become Vocabulary words themselves: each sentence of a long answer, or of a paragraph, is translated (a card with a one-sentence answer stays a Speaking card only), Vietnamese into English and anything else into Vietnamese; the preview counts them as 🔤 from sentences and the status line says how many were added. English or Vietnamese under Translate to splits sentences the same way and fills in a translation as the answer. Bullets in front of a line are dropped with every delimiter.
- **Questions and answers without a video go to Vocabulary.** Lessons are for studying with YouTube: a pasted card whose answer translates its question (the two are in different languages) and that has no video time becomes a word and its meaning in Vocabulary instead of a card, also when made with Translate to. When a new lesson ends up with only words, it becomes a Vocabulary lesson and opens there. Cards with a video time, and questions answered in the same language (the cards studied with Speaking), stay in the lesson, and Speaking never asks to move a card.
- Start a line with a timestamp (`1:23 question : answer`) and set the lesson's YouTube link to tie each card to a moment in the video.
- **New things go on top.** Every way of adding, one at a time or pasted in bulk, puts the new cards at the top of the lesson and the new words at the top of the word list (a pasted batch keeps its own order). The ＋ on the Add cards page opens ☰ Full with the new card at the top, and the ＋ on Add vocabularies opens ☰ List with the new word at the top.
- Each card has rich-text notes. Lines written as `word: meaning` become words in the vocabulary deck. A word made from the card's own sentence (select part of the question, or of the answer in Speaking, and press Shift, in the editor or on the Speaking paper) first opens a small `word: meaning` box, everywhere alike; nothing is added until you fill in the meaning and press Return (Esc closes it, so a stray Shift adds nothing). The word then goes into the notes (in the editor, into its Notes box, saved with the card) in the order the words come in the sentence: make the fourth word first and then the first, and the first goes above the fourth. Words that are not in the sentence (typed or pasted yourself) are left where they are, and a new word not in the sentence goes at the end. A word can be any length and may contain colons, so a whole sentence such as `Second: don't brush too hard.` moves to Vocabulary as it is, with its meaning.
- Questions, answers and notes keep only basic formatting: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, line breaks and bullet or numbered lists. Anything else pasted in (font sizes, colours, fonts, headings, links, images) is dropped, so a paste from a web page, Word or Google Docs cannot change how a card looks.
- Lessons can be saved as `.json` files and opened again, one at a time or all at once.

## Studying

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| ☰ Full | The whole lesson as a list, for browsing and editing. No spaced repetition. Above the list it shows the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ (New, Due, Done) counts, like the study modes, each count lit up when it is not zero (hidden once everything is studied). A ＋ bar at the top of the list, inside it as its header like the bar of ☰ List in Vocabulary (the list has no Question / Answer header row) (once the lesson has cards; a new, empty lesson shows the add form with its own ＋, and a lesson whose last card is moved or deleted opens Add cards, with the lesson picked, as Vocabulary opens Add vocabularies when its last word goes), in the logo's colour, shows how many cards the lesson has in front of the ＋ (such as 97 ＋) and adds a card at the top of the list (the same as the count and ＋ on the Vocabulary word list, at the same height). In a text lesson, clicking a card (its question or its answer) copies the whole card, question and answer together, in one click, and pointing at either part shades both. In a text lesson the list is a notebook page: white ruled paper with a red margin (on a phone there is no margin line, and the text sits a little further in from both edges), each question as a heading in the lesson colour with its answer written on the lines below, and the editor puts the short question on one line above a large answer box, without a dictionary. No table has a column of ✎, ✕ or 字 buttons, on a computer or a phone, in Lessons or in Vocabulary: point at a card (or select it on a phone) and press E to edit it, Delete to delete it or, in a text lesson, V to move it to Vocabulary, flying off as the logo (on a phone the bar of copy, ✎, ✕ and 字 described below does the same): the question becomes a word, the answer its meaning, and words filed under the card go along; if the sentence is already a word, the card just leaves the lesson. Undo brings it back. In every editor a coloured line starts each box: blue for the question, green for the answer, grey for the notes, and the answer box fits its text. |
| Group | Due cards three at a time (the last two groups of a session are balanced, so 4 left become 2 + 2), each group listed in video-time order (0:05, 0:10, 0:15), so its clips play like a stretch of the video. Shown in video lessons. The three are laid out as a chat, not a table, in iMessage bubbles: each card's question is a grey bubble on the left (with its time badge) and its answer a blue bubble answering it on the right, each with its small curled tail; a side that is not shown yet is a bubble on one line with three round dots, like iMessage's typing bubble but standing still (after the time badge on a question). There is no coloured strip for the card that is playing, and nothing fades: its time badge fills with the lesson colour. Only on a phone, the card you pick stays bright while the others fade back. All of it is in plain type (weight 400), on a computer and on a phone alike. The bubbles sit straight on the page, with no card or frame around them, evenly spaced, one under another, not set apart in question-and-answer pairs, so a group fits on one screen. The chat bubbles and the Listening list use 20 px (18 px on a phone), so a whole group fits on one screen at 100 %; both sides of a flashcard 28 px (24 px on a phone); a question and its answer are always the same size, in every mode (the Speaking paper 20 px, on a computer and on a phone alike; the ☰ Full notebook 20 px), with whole-pixel line heights everywhere. The phone's browser is told not to enlarge text by itself. Click the notes (tap them on a phone) to edit them right there, like the answer (also tapped on a phone): Return saves, Shift + Return starts a new line, Esc cancels, and undo brings the old notes back. The group of three works the sound as before (▶ plays the group, 1 – 3 loops one card). There are no dictionary panels: once the group is shown, each sentence that has Chinese characters gets its own line of stroke order under the bubbles (three sentences, three lines), with no card or frame around it, the lines evenly spaced; they appear only when the group is shown, and a character can be tapped (clicked) to draw it again. The dictionary is in the card editor (E or ✎), under Save, with the same even spacing as the rest of the editor. Clicking it loops that sentence, or stops it when it is the one looping, and it lights up while the sentence plays. In the table too, a card without video has 🔊 in front of its question, as in Vocabulary's Group, to read it aloud over and over (click again to stop). |
| Listening | Cards come in groups, by the same rule as Group (three at a time, the last two balanced, each group in video-time order), laid out straight on the page, with no card or frame around them, like a track list: each card is a line with a round time button on the left (0:05; round, roomy enough for times such as 14:35, and widened into a pill only for a long one such as 1:02:30, the lines of a batch starting together after the widest button; tap it to play that clip over and over, as the time on a Group bubble does, and tap it again to stop; a line without a clip has a 🔊 button that reads it aloud; it fills with the lesson colour and pulses while it plays, with no coloured strip and no fading), its question and, under it, its answer in green on the right, both in the same size and plain (not bold) type, the lines divided by thin rules. Before they are shown, the question and the answer are grey bars about as long as the hidden text, with a soft shimmer, so you can tell how much you are listening for. The clips play first, in turn; Space shows all three, and then, as in Group, each line with Chinese characters gets its own line of stroke order underneath, with no frame; the dictionary is in the card editor (point at or tap a line for its pinyin); grading grades all three (or just the card you point at, which then flies off on its own), Delete deletes all three (or the card you point at), E edits the card you point at, and the copy button copies all three. On a phone, once the lines are shown, tap a line to pick it (the other lines fade back, and a second tap lets it go): the copy and ✕ bar then acts on that line alone, and on all three when no line is picked. The three stay together until each is graded; a session's last card can come on its own. Shown when the lesson has video cards. |
| 🗒️ Speaking | Laid out as an exam paper, one question at a time, for speaking practice of any kind: a white ruled sheet headed with the lesson's name, Question 2 of 5 and a clock that runs while you answer out loud; the heading stays in the same place when the answer is shown, so nothing jumps. The question is numbered, in the lesson colour; the ruled lines below it show ···, and the answer comes in two steps, the same with a click (or tap) on the paper, Space, Return or the phone's button (which reads Hint, then Show): the first writes a hint on those lines, the meanings of the lesson's words in the answer, in the order they come, without the words themselves, in the paper's normal ink, numbered with the numbers lined up on the left (the same in Notes); the second writes the answer there. A question without notes skips the hint and shows the answer at once. The clock is not clickable. Before the answer is shown the hint is the only help. A text lesson (the kind studied with Speaking) shows no pinyin or reading when you point at (or tap) its questions and answers, anywhere: not on the Speaking paper and not in the ☰ Full notebook. The answer then comes on the left, with the lesson's words highlighted like a highlighter pen, and a numbered Notes list of those words and meanings on the right, divided from it by a red line; Notes takes only as much width as its lines need (up to a little under half the paper), so short notes leave the answer more room and long ones are not broken over two lines without need, and each note sits a little apart from the next (on a phone, Notes comes under the answer); the clock stops. As in Listening, once the answer is shown it stays until you grade: Space, a click on the paper and the phone's button (greyed out) do nothing more. ← or → grades, as everywhere. The paper takes the normal dark background in the dark theme. Everything the question does elsewhere, the answer does here: once the answer is shown, the foot of the paper, across its whole width under the answer and Notes, shows the stroke order of its characters and the dictionary for it (there is no separate panel; the paper's sections sit 32 px apart and its dictionary is 360 px tall, like the others, the dictionary moves with the page as you scroll, and the dictionaries of the current and next questions load while the question is still showing). Pointing at the answer keeps its ruled lines and shows a hand, since clicking it opens the card editor, as in Listening, and selecting part of the answer on the paper and pressing Shift adds it as a word. On a computer there are no buttons for the card: E edits it, Delete deletes it and V moves it to Vocabulary; on a phone copy, ✎, ✕ and 字 are in the bar above the bottom buttons. There is no sound button, and Space does nothing once the answer is shown. In a text lesson the card editor does the same with the answer box: it highlights the words, Shift adds a selected part as a word, and the dictionary under it translates the answer. Shown in lessons without a video; it uses the same spaced repetition as Group. |

In a video lesson the video sits in a player bar as wide as the lesson, fully transparent, also while it is pinned over the cards: the video plays in the small picture on the left, beside its title and a progress bar you can drag with the time and length. Click the picture and the video grows out of it, with a genie effect, into a large player as wide as the lesson, shown in full in every mode (Listening covers only the small picture); the button in its corner shrinks it back into the bar. The bar stays pinned at the top as you scroll; the large player scrolls away with the page while the mode buttons stay pinned, and making it large (with the picture or Y) scrolls the page back up to it. ▶ and the speed beside the mode buttons work as before. The mode buttons have no tooltips.

**On a phone,** as on a computer, tables have only the question and answer columns, so they get the whole width. Every embedded page (each dictionary, in every mode, and the video) works at the first touch, with no selecting first and no outline: swiping over a dictionary scrolls the dictionary's own page, and once it reaches the end the whole page carries on scrolling. In a Group, as in Listening, the first tap on a card shows the group; once it is shown, tap a card to select it. Tapping the same card twice in quick succession opens it for editing, in Group, Listening, Speaking (anywhere on the paper, before or after the answer is shown) and ☰ Full, and on a word in Vocabulary's ☰ List. The card editor opens right where the card was, at the same height on the screen, without scrolling; in Listening only the line being edited turns into the editor and the other lines stay in place. Tap a row to select it: a bar of copy (two overlapping pages), ✕ and 字, tinted in the lesson colour like the mode buttons, (字 only where it is offered on a computer) appears, centred, just above the bottom bar of undo, redo and the study buttons, and does exactly what C and Delete do on a computer. In Group and Listening it is always there: with nothing tapped it acts on the whole group or the three lines, and once a card or line is tapped on that one alone. On a flashcard and the Speaking paper it is always there and acts on the card showing. In ☰ Full and ☰ List it shows only once a card or word is tapped, and acts on that one. Only the flashcard's bar has ✎ (a tap there flips the card); everywhere else, the Speaking paper included, tapping twice opens the editor, so there is no ✎. Copy, on the left, copies as C does: the questions only, as an A. B. C. list when there are several (a single one is copied as it is); a word on its own (without its meaning) in Vocabulary; in ☰ Full the card as a click on it copies it. On a flashcard and the Speaking paper this bar stands in for buttons in the toolbar, so nothing sits at the top.

A lesson is either a video lesson (it has a YouTube, TikTok or Douyin link, or cards with video times), studied with Group and Listening, or a text lesson, studied with Speaking; adding or removing the link switches between them, also in the middle of a session. Group and Listening share one queue: a card studied in one mode leaves the others until it is due again.

**Sound.** The YouTube button (or Space, once the cards are shown) plays the cards' video clips, at the speed beside it. Cards without a clip are read aloud in their turn, so clips and reading alternate; in a lesson without a video the button becomes 🔊 and reads the questions aloud. The speed sits beside the button whether it plays clips or reads aloud, in Lessons and in Vocabulary; reading aloud has its own speed, 1x to start, and clips theirs, 1.5x to start, and the box shows the one in use. While sound plays, the button shows a pause mark (the YouTube mark with ⏸ bars for clips, ⏸ for reading) and is filled with the mode's colour; press it again to stop. Sound also stops at once, mid-word, when you grade or move on, switch mode, lesson or view, or switch between Lessons and Vocabulary. Showing a group, or showing or flipping a word in Vocabulary, plays it automatically.

**Out of the way while you study.** In every study mode (Group, Listening and Speaking in Lessons; Flashcards in Vocabulary) the lesson's name, Save, Reset and Delete are hidden, with the language and video link in Lessons; they come back in ☰ Full and ☰ List, and when everything is studied. What is pinned in a study mode (the video bar and the mode buttons in Lessons, the toolbar in Vocabulary) sits 6 px below the top of the window from the start and stays exactly there as you scroll, so nothing slides up first; on a phone it sits 6 px under the bar with the logo, also when the video is large. The same 6 px is used at the bottom on a phone: the buttons of the bottom bar sit 6 px from the bottom edge and from the bar's top line, and the copy ✎ ✕ 字 bar sits 6 px above it. ☰ Full and ☰ List keep their space above. In every study mode (Group, Listening, Speaking and Flashcards) the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ counts sit 16 px under the mode buttons and the first thing under them (the first bubble, the first Listening line, the Speaking paper, the flashcard) starts 16 px below the counts.

**No grey text.** Everything you read while studying (column headings, the ··· of hidden fields, the progress line on a card, and on the Speaking paper its heading, question count, clock and Notes label) is in the normal text colour.

**Back to the card.** Revealing or flipping a card brings the study area to the top in one step, in Lessons and in Vocabulary. Moving on to the next card or group never scrolls the page, even if you graded it far down (say, from the bottom of a long group): the page stays where it is and the next card simply starts right under the pinned bar, where you are already looking, so nothing jumps even though an unanswered card is shorter. The space this leaves above it is taken away by itself, without anything moving, the moment you next touch the screen, turn the mouse wheel or press a key, before the page starts to move, so scrolling back up afterwards is smooth. Grading, swiping or deleting a single line (in a Group or in Listening) keeps your view exactly where it is: what you were looking at stays at the same spot on the screen, even when a line above it goes, and the page does not grow to do it.

**Scheduling.** *Again* brings a card back in 10 minutes. Each *Good* counts towards a streak; until the fifth Good in a row the card returns the next day. After five it is **done**, and the gaps grow to 1, 3 and then 7 days.

**Lessons in the sidebar.** They are shown as a compact grid of folders, two per row, in Lessons and Vocabulary alike (there is no list view and no switch). Each folder has its own background and outline, so it stands out without pointing at it. On the cover is the lesson's YouTube thumbnail, or, without one, as many of its first words as fit in large type, filling the cover and centred, never cut off with an ellipsis. The name sits under the cover on up to two lines (cut short with … when it is longer), and under the name a thin bar is split by share of cards: New (green), Due (orange), still being learned and not yet due (light grey) and Done; the bars of folders side by side line up, even when one name takes a single line; point at a folder to see its full name and exact counts with their icons (total | 🆕 ⏳ ✅). Adding cards or words goes through the ＋ above the list (or ⌘⌥A); the sidebar has no Add button. Open file, Back up, Statistics and Theme sit at the bottom of the sidebar with no dividing line above them, on a block tinted lightly in the logo's colour (blue in Lessons, green in Vocabulary). When there is nothing to show, no lessons in Lessons or no words in Vocabulary, the sidebar is hidden and the page shows the same welcome screen in both (📚, Welcome to Flashcards, + New lesson and Open file), with the logo at the top to switch between Lessons and Vocabulary.

**List first.** Opening a lesson, switching between Lessons and Vocabulary, or going to another page (Add cards, Statistics…) and coming back always lands on the list: ☰ Full in Lessons, ☰ List in Vocabulary, never straight into a study mode. A group you were in the middle of is kept: choosing Group again carries on where you left off.

**Corners.** Rounding is kept modest and tells the parts apart: cards, tables, popups and dialogs 12 px (the most anywhere); what sits inside them (dictionaries, stroke-order boxes, the small video, the bottom buttons, the mode switch) 10 px; long bars, buttons and fields (+ New lesson, search, the ＋ bar) 8 px; small details (mode buttons, highlights, the pinyin box, time badges) 6–8 px. The Speaking paper keeps its nearly square sheet corners. Whatever flies off when you swipe is outlined exactly at its own edges and corners, and only the study content flies (never the video, the counts or the mode buttons). One card graded on its own flies alone: its row (its stroke-order line simply goes at once, without fading, so nothing lingers as a ghost). In Group and Listening every card goes the same way, the last one of a group included: grading, swiping or deleting a card flies just its line, its stroke-order line simply goes, and only then does the next group come. While you drag a card's line sideways, only that line and its stroke-order line disappear from under your finger, leaving their space, so nothing else moves or shrinks behind it; this is the same for the last card left as for the others. When a whole group goes at once (grading or deleting all of it together), or a Speaking card, everything that belongs to it flies off together as one card, outlined from the top of the table (or Listening card, or Speaking paper) to the bottom of its last line of stroke order: the video, the counts and the mode buttons above are not part of it. So you see it fly wherever you have scrolled to, with Good or Again in the middle of the part on screen.

**The fairy.** The little fairy from Reset (bun, blue dress, see-through wings, star-tipped wand) turns up for a few other moments, never for everyday grading, always with the same fine, many-coloured glowing dust:
- **Light ☀️ / dark 🌙:** she flies to the Theme button and taps the air with her wand; the new colours spread out from the wand's tip in a growing circle until they cover the screen.
- **Undo of a Reset (⌘Z):** the same as a Reset, but the other way: she comes in from the right and sprinkles the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ counts from right to left, the dust covering them in that direction, and they come back with their old numbers. Redo is a Reset again, from the left. Redo sprinkles them again like a Reset.
- **Deleting a whole lesson:** she touches it with her wand and it melts into fairy dust (the lesson in the lesson list, or its name when the list is tucked away). A deleted card or word flies off marked ✕, as always.
- **+ New lesson** and **opening lesson files (Open file):** she sprinkles the new lessons in the lesson list (or the lesson's name), and they light up.
- **Adding a video link** (Open, or a link in pasted cards): she floats along the new video bar, sprinkling it.
- **Save, 💾 Back up and signing in to sync:** she taps the Save, Back up or cloud button, which lights up, once the file is saved or you are signed in.
- **A video that is still loading** when you press play: she flies round the small video until it is ready (at most ten seconds), instead of a "still loading" message.
- **Switching between Lesson and Vocabulary:** she flies right across the screen (to the right into Vocabulary, to the left back to Lessons), leaving a trail of fairy dust, as the other side slides in.
With reduced motion turned on in the system, she stays away and things simply happen.

**Done is done.** When nothing is left to study, the study modes are hidden and the lesson goes back to ☰ Full: 🎉 sits above the list, with confetti, without the New, Due and Done counts (the same in Vocabulary). There is no button to study ahead; the modes come back when cards are due again. (**Reset** makes a whole lesson New again, if you want to start it over, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike; to show it happened, a little fairy flies in: a tiny girl with her hair in a bun, a blue dress and see-through wings, glowing softly, comes in from the left waving her star-tipped wand, then floats slowly along the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ counts from left to right, from the first to the last, bobbing gently and sprinkling fine fairy dust from the wand's tip: soft glowing specks in many colours (gold, pink, blue, lilac, mint) with a few tiny twinkling stars, which spreads out from her first touch and, little by little over about a second, covers the whole 🆕 ⏳ ✅ cluster in a soft cloud with rounded ends, the counts slowly fading away beneath it, and keeps falling thick under her as she floats along; the specks shimmer gently instead of flickering; when she reaches the end the counts come back through the settling dust already showing their new numbers (no counting up or down), in the counts row and in the lesson list alike, and she flies off the other side, all in about three and a half seconds. She visits the counts on screen (☰ Full and ☰ List show them too, so after a reset they are right there), or, if none can be seen, the lesson's counts in the lesson list or the cards that have just become New. The reset is saved and synced like a review: reloading the page or opening the lesson on another device keeps it New, and only a review made after the reset counts over it.)

### Keyboard

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| ← or Shift + Return | Again (while the card or group is still hidden, Return and Shift + Return show it first, like Space) |
| → or Return | Good (likewise, Return shows a hidden card first) |
| C | Copy the questions being studied: the whole group or the three Listening lines, or only the card or line you point at or have selected |
| E | Edit a card: the one being studied in Speaking, or in Group, Listening and ☰ Full the card or line you point at or have selected |
| 1 – 3 | In Group and Listening, play just that card over and over: its video clip, or read aloud when it has none (press again to stop). |
| Space | In Lessons, show, one way only: once the group or card is shown it is never hidden again, and from then on Space is the sound button beside the mode buttons (with the rule the old S key had): whatever is playing stops (the whole group, or a row looping from 1 – 3), and when nothing is playing it plays the group; if the sound was meant to be playing but nothing can be heard (the video stalled, or the browser dropped the reading), it starts it again with one press instead of first switching off the silence. In Speaking it goes hint, then answer, and then does nothing more (Speaking has no sound). In Vocabulary, show or flip, both ways; the sound plays by itself when a group is shown or a card is flipped, and the 🔊 button turns it on or off. The phone's Show button does the same and names what it will do: Show (Hint first in Speaking), then ▶ or ⏸ once a group or Listening card is shown (greyed out in Speaking); ⟲ in Flashcards. In Vocabulary, Again and Good read ✕ and ✓, on the buttons and on the card that flies off when you grade or swipe |
| ⌘/Ctrl + Z | Undo, including grades (add Shift to redo). Undo and Redo go back to exactly that moment: the same mode, the same group or Listening set, the same cards shown or hidden, the same Speaking question and the same side of a flashcard, so a card brought back is the one you were on, not moved to a later turn |
| V | Move a card of a text lesson to Vocabulary: the one being studied in Speaking, or in a table the row you point at or have selected |
| Delete or Backspace | Delete the card; it flies off marked ✕. In a group, pointing at a card's row deletes that card and the row flies off (in Listening, pointing at a line, that line), and the page keeps your place, the same as grading. Nothing happens while a card is open for editing, or when a Vietnamese typing tool (Telex, VNI) sends Backspace to turn a letter into ê, đ, é and so on |
| Shift (with text selected on the Speaking paper) | Select part of the answer to add it as a word |
| Esc | Close a popup |
| M | Next study mode (☰ → Group / Listening / Speaking in Lessons, ☰ → 🗃️ in Vocabulary) |
| F | In Vocabulary, switch which side shows first (⇄) |
| [ and ] | Slower or faster sound (the speed beside the sound button) |
| Y | Make the video large or put it back in the bar; when it grows, the page scrolls back up to it |
| ⌘⌥A (Ctrl+Alt+A) | Add a card or word (the ＋ in ☰ Full or ☰ List; elsewhere it opens Add cards or Add vocabularies) |
| ⌘⌥N (Ctrl+Alt+N) | New lesson. The sidebar's shortcuts (N, O, B, W, Q, T) and A need ⌘ and ⌥ on a Mac, Ctrl and Alt elsewhere, so they are not pressed by accident |
| ⌘⌥O (Ctrl+Alt+O) | Open a file (the same as Open file) |
| ⌘⌥B (Ctrl+Alt+B) | Back up |
| ⌘⌥W (Ctrl+Alt+W) | Statistics |
| ⌘⌥Q (Ctrl+Alt+Q) | Switch between Lessons and Vocabulary |
| ⌘⌥T (Ctrl+Alt+T) | Switch between the light and dark theme |
| / | Jump to the lesson search box (on a phone it opens the menu first) |
| ⌘/Ctrl + S | Save the lesson as a .json file, like Save (add Shift to pick a different file); works even while typing |

The same keys work in every study mode, in Lessons and in Vocabulary. In a Lesson group, pointing at a row makes the grading keys grade just that card.

On a phone, swipe a card right for Good and left for Again. Whatever flies off (a card's line, a card, a whole group) is outlined exactly at its own edges, with its own rounded corners (a line flies as a rounded strip). In a Lesson group, and in Listening while it holds more than one card, swiping one card's line (its two bubbles, or its line in the Listening list) grades just that card; swiping anywhere else grades the whole group. While you swipe, what comes next is already shown underneath, in the same form it will have: the next lines or the next group's chat, the next Listening card, the next Speaking paper (its next question number and a fresh clock), and the next flashcard.

## Vocabulary

Switch from **Lessons** to **Vocabulary** with the 字 button at the top. Words come from the `word: meaning` lines in your notes, or can be pasted in directly. They have their own review sessions and scheduling, one lesson at a time or all words together.

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| ☰ List | Every word of the lesson, for browsing and editing, with the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ (New, Due, Done) counts above it like the other modes, each lit up when it is not zero (hidden once every word is studied): the bar at the top shows the number of words and, with ＋, adds a word; point at a word (or tap it on a phone) to see its pinyin or reading, and press E to edit it or Delete to delete it. No grading. |
| 🗃️ Flashcards | One word at a time; tap the card to flip it sideways (except on the stroke order, which you can tap to draw a character again). Front and back are the same size, and the card is only as tall as what is on it (the taller of the two sides, at least 200 px), so it does not leave a lot of empty space; it never grows past what the screen allows: once the toolbar is pinned at the top, at most down to 40 px above the bottom bars (or the bottom of the window), on a phone held upright as on a computer. It refits when the window or the card's content changes (always a whole, even number of pixels), and the page below it ends there too, so there is nothing to scroll down to. The card keeps that size when it turns, so nothing grows or jumps after the flip; if the back ever holds more than fits (very long notes), it scrolls inside the card, starting at the top each time. There is no dictionary on a flashcard: the back holds the sentence the word comes from with the word highlighted and its meaning, the word's notes, and the stroke order, evenly spaced, with the word highlighted. On both sides the whole block is centred on the card, with as much space above it as below (also when the reading is shown over the sentence). On the back, sentence, meaning, notes and stroke order are evenly spaced, and the space above the first and below the last is never less than that (25 px): a card that fits shows it centred, and a back that is too long scrolls inside the card with the same 25 px at its top and at its end. On a phone, copy, ✎ and ✕ for the word sit in the bar above the bottom buttons, never on the card; on a computer, E edits and Delete deletes the word. The next word's card is prepared out of sight underneath, so it is ready when it comes up. Turning the card back does not scroll the page to the top. |

The mode buttons have no tooltips, and Vocabulary has no dictionary button. 🔊 reads the sentence that really holds the word (also a sentence inside a long answer), or the word itself when no sentence has it.

Editing a word (E, ✎, or ＋ for a new one) opens the same three boxes as a card in Lessons, with the same coloured lines: Question (blue), Answer (green) and Notes (grey). Notes take anything you type or paste, formatting included, with no look-ups or highlighting; Return saves (Shift + Return for a new line) and undo works as usual. A word's notes show on the back of its flashcard, under the meaning, and stay with the word when it is renamed, reset, or moved over from a lesson.

In Flashcards a word marked Again comes back after 10 minutes. The toolbar stays pinned to the top of the screen as you scroll, in both modes, as in Lessons. The 🌐 button picks the language; ⇄ picks which side is shown first and hides the other side again (the card gives a short squash, and the side it starts on is coloured as a question or an answer). No list or study mode carries the words Question and Answer: ☰ Full is headed by its ＋ bar and ☰ List by its own, and the Group chat, the Listening card, the Speaking paper and the flashcards show no such labels, questions and answers being told apart by their colour. On a phone, Undo, Redo, Show, Again and Good sit in a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen, as in Lessons; on a computer the keys do the same.

As in Lessons, pointing at a word (or tapping its row on a phone) shows its pinyin or reading.

When every word is studied, 🗃️ and ⇄ are hidden: only 🌐, the counts, 🎉 and the word list, always open, remain until words are due again.

Words already in your vocabulary (the `word: meaning` lines in notes, in any lesson) are highlighted wherever a lesson's cards are shown, in every mode and on a computer or a phone alike: the Group bubbles, the Listening lines, the Speaking paper and the ☰ Full list, as well as the card editor (Question, Answer and Notes). Green means another card, in this lesson or another one, also has the word: click (or tap) it, in the editor or right where you are studying, to see that card's sentence and note, with the lesson's name when it comes from another lesson (cards from the lesson you are in come first). The popup is as wide as its text (up to 320 px), with the same space above and below the text and on both sides; the side space only widens to make room for the ‹ › arrows when there are several matches. Inside the popup, point at the sentence, at the highlighted word in it, or at a word in the note (tap it on a phone; tap again to hide) to see its pinyin or reading, in the same small box as in the table, but light with dark text so it stands out on the dark popup. Grey means only this card has it, and clicking it does nothing special. Clicking a green word only opens this popup; clicking anywhere else on the card does what it always does (opens the card editor, selects or copies the card). Every word is highlighted, even inside or across another one, with the colours layered where they share characters; pointing at a word lights up all of it.

Words are found by the kind of writing at their edges, with no language setting and no lists of words to skip:

- Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese, written without spaces, match anywhere.
- Korean must start a spaced chunk but may end inside it, so 학교 matches 학교에서 but not 대학교.
- Languages written with spaces (English, Vietnamese, Russian…) must match whole words, so "cat" does not match "concatenate".
- Japanese verbs and adjectives also match in any form: 食べる highlights 食べました and 食べない. A word that covers part of a kanji compound highlights the whole compound, so its furigana stays in one piece.
- English words also match their other forms: -s, -es, -ies, -ed, -ied and -ing (cats, watches, studied, stopped, running, making), common irregular verbs, plurals and comparisons (went for go, children for child, better for good), and each word of a phrase (gave up for give up). In a phrase, a, an and the stand for each other and may be left out (a travel pass highlights the travel pass and travel passes, issue a travel pass highlights issued travel passes), sb, sth, someone and something stand for one to three words (give sb a hand highlights gave my brother a hand), one's, your, his… stand for any of them (make up one's mind highlights made up her mind), oneself for myself, himself…, and a hyphen counts as a space (well-known and well known). A highlight means the text holds a real form of the word, and you decide whether the meaning fits (left is a form of leave even in turn left). Look-alikes that are not forms are never matched: -er, -est and -ly are left out (corner, forest, only), and evening, thing or seed are not read as forms of even, the or see.
- Korean verbs and adjectives written with -다 match their usual conjugations: 먹다 highlights 먹어요 and 먹었어요, 가다 highlights 가요, 갔어요 and 갑니다, 보다 highlights 봐요, and 공부하다 highlights 공부해요 and 공부했어요. The common irregular verbs match too: 덥다 → 더워요, 돕다 → 도와요, 듣다 → 들어요, 낫다 → 나아요, 모르다 → 몰라요, 그렇다 → 그래요, 바쁘다 → 바빠요. A form can belong to more than one word (가요 is also a noun, 들어요 also comes from 들다); it is highlighted for each, and you decide which fits.
- Full-width and half-width forms count as the same (ｶﾀｶﾅ and カタカナ, ＡＢＣ and ABC), katakana and hiragana count as the same (ネコ and ねこ), Vietnamese tone marks may sit on either vowel (hòa and hoà, khỏe and khoẻ, thúy and thuý), case does not matter, and line breaks count as spaces.
