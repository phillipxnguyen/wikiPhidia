# wikiPhidia

Flashcards for learning Chinese and Japanese, with spaced repetition, YouTube clips, dictionaries and a vocabulary deck built from your notes.

The app is called **Flashcard**, and its icon is a white 字 on a blue rounded square, the same as the mark in its top bar: it shows in the browser tab and on bookmarks, and becomes the app's name and icon on a phone's home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen on an iPhone, Install app on Android), where it opens on its own, without the browser's bars. The tab reads the lesson's name followed by · Flashcard.

The whole app is one file, `index.html`. There is no build step and nothing to install: open it in a browser, or host it anywhere that serves static files. On a phone it can be added to the home screen and runs full screen.

## Lessons and cards

- **One name.** The app is called **Flashcard** beside its 字 logo, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike (only its colour changes, blue or green); clicking it switches between the two.
- **Big writing in every box you type cards into.** The card editor (Question, Answer, Notes), the ＋ boxes for a single card or word, Vocabulary's editor and the small box for a new word follow the same sizes as the cards outside them: the question and answer (word and meaning) at 20 px on 28 px lines, the notes a little smaller at 18 px on 25 px lines, so editing a card looks like the card itself; lists in them are indented to match. The paste box (Paste here!) is not a card and keeps its usual size, like the other boxes around it: 14 px on 20 px lines (16 px on 24 px on a phone). Every list follows the Speaking paper: the question and the answer are the same size, 20 px, and the notes a little smaller, 18 px on 25 px lines. So in ☰ Full the sentence of a video lesson is 20 px with its notes at 18 px, as the question, answer and notes of a text lesson's notebook already were, and in Vocabulary's ☰ List (and All vocabularies) the word and its meaning are 20 px while the notes under the meaning, in the same column, are 18 px. On a phone everything is one step smaller in the same proportion: the question and answer 18 px on 25 px lines and the notes 16 px on 22 px, in the editors, the lists, the notebook page and the Speaking paper (whose ruled lines are 32 px apart there), and the notebook page keeps the same room on both sides of the writing (about 25 px from the paper's edge); in ☰ Full the time badge and the sentence start 17 px in from the card's left edge (21 px on a computer), the notes' bullets start 6 px inside the sentence's left edge, while on the right the notes stop where the sentence stops (17 px before the card's edge on a phone, 21 px on a computer); the bullets of the notebook page and of Vocabulary's ☰ List sit 6 px in from their text the same way, their right edge again that of the text, and a plain line of notes starts in line with the bullets, and Vocabulary's ☰ List rows keep 16 px on both sides. Boxes that are already large, such as the search box, the lesson's name and the link box, stay as they are.
- **The flag.** In front of the lesson's name, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike, its left edge in line with the link box and the cards below and centred on the name's line, a real flag (from the flag-icons set, built into the app) waves on a small pole with a gold knob (every flag in the app, here and on the toolbars, is the same 32 × 24 px cloth, the flags' own 4 : 3 shape so the picture fills it edge to edge, sized to sit well beside the 36 px sound button; several languages hang together from that one pole: a rope leaves its top nearly level, and each flag, as big as a lone one (32 × 24 px), upright and 4 px from the next, is tied to it at a point of its left edge picked at random and sways about it on its own, free to wave over whatever is around it: the pole keeps its place and the name stays where it always is, the rope simply running out over it, the main language nearest the pole; while you edit the name it comes up in front of the flags, on its white box, so you see what you type): the whole cloth swings and a soft fold of light runs across it, so it never breaks into strips. Click it to pick the language (Auto-detect, Simplified, Traditional, Japanese, Korean, English or Vietnamese). With Auto-detect it shows the language most of the lesson's cards are in, and while a card is being studied the language of that card; where there is no single language (an empty lesson, All lessons, All vocabularies with several languages) it shows several smaller flags on their own poles waving side by side, one per language (up to three; 🇬🇧 🇨🇳 🇻🇳 when nothing tells). There is no flag behind the page: every page keeps the plain page colour. With reduced motion turned on the flag does not wave.
- **Clean spacing everywhere.** One shared cleaner tidies every text that comes in or is saved: pasted and imported cards, translations, YouTube subtitles, lesson files and backups, the card editor, words and meanings, lesson names. Words are never separated by more than one space (tabs, non-breaking and wide spaces count as spaces too), lines start and end with no space, and Chinese and Japanese characters have no space between them (今天  我们 去 becomes 今天我们去), while Korean keeps its single spaces between words. Lessons saved before are tidied the same way when they are opened.
- Paste cards into a lesson, one per line, as `question : answer` or tab-separated. The separator is detected and a preview shows what will be imported. Column delimiter offers Auto-detect, Tab and Colon ( : ), in Lessons and Vocabulary alike; only Tab also takes a time column and a notes column. **Auto-detect**, the first choice and the default on both sides, reads a whole study text in one paste, in Lessons or in Vocabulary alike: a question line just above a paragraph (ending in ?, starting with Q: or Question:, or a short heading such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes one card with the whole paragraph as its answer (an A: or Answer: in front is dropped; following paragraphs belong to the same answer), any other paragraph becomes one question-only card per sentence, each `question = answer` or `question: answer` line (with or without a `*`, `-`, `•` or `1.` in front) becomes a word, a line with Tab columns is read column by column as under Tab (time, question, answer, notes; it is never taken as the lesson title), and a short line just above a word list (such as `🔥 Phrasal verbs to conquer today`) is left out. Each word is filed under the card whose question or answer uses it, written exactly the same (case, spaces and punctuation aside), so clicking it there shows that sentence, and the back of its flashcard shows the sentence with that form highlighted (the back always uses a sentence of the lesson that contains the word, even if the word was filed under another card); a word no sentence uses goes to the lesson's own word list. A short first line without sentence punctuation (such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes the lesson title when the lesson is empty (with Import and with Translate to alike), and stays the question of its card when a paragraph follows it. A dialogue (lines such as `Patient: …` and `Dentist: …`, where the same speakers come back) is kept as it is under Auto-detect: each line is one card with its speaker, never split at the colon, and a line ending in ? is not taken as the question of the line under it. From Auto-detect, pasted cards or words still switch to Tab or colon by themselves when more than half the lines use it and no line holds several sentences (a colon after a finished sentence does not count, nor do Q:, A:, Question: and Answer: labels, so a paste of short Q: and A: lines stays questions and answers); from any other choice, Auto-detect is picked when no line has a Tab or colon. When a paste into a text lesson (from Lessons or Vocabulary) has no word list, its sentences become Vocabulary words themselves: each sentence of a long answer, or of a paragraph, is translated (a card with a one-sentence answer stays a Speaking card only), Vietnamese into English and anything else into Vietnamese; the preview counts them as 🔤 from sentences and the status line says how many were added. English or Vietnamese under Translate to splits sentences the same way and fills in a translation as the answer. Bullets in front of a line are dropped with every delimiter. A time in front of a line becomes the card's video time with every delimiter, also in brackets (`[4:53]` or `(4:53)`) and also when it stands on a line of its own above its sentence, as in a transcript copied from YouTube (`4:53` on one line, the sentence on the next). A lesson made earlier whose cards still carry such times at the start of their text (most of its cards, at least three) gets them back as video times when it is opened: the time leaves the question (its formatting kept), and the answer too when it starts with the same time; those cards keep their progress and are not merged.
- **Sentences come in threes.** Short subtitle lines are joined into longer stretches to study in one go: in every lesson with timed cards (from a video or YouTube subtitles), the cards are taken in time order from 0:00 to the end and every three in a row become one card. Its sentences follow each other (with no space between them in Chinese or Japanese) and so do their translations, it keeps the time of the first sentence (so its clip runs through all three), its notes are joined into one list, and it starts as a new card. The words in those notes keep their own progress in Vocabulary, which works by word and is unchanged. This happens once for the lessons you already have (when the app starts or a lesson is opened) and right away for every new import; a last one or two left over stay as they are, and a joined card is never joined again. It concerns Listening and ☰ Full; text lessons studied with 🗒️ Speaking have no times and are left alone.
- **Video cards have no answer.** A card with a video time is only its sentence and its notes: Listening studies it by ear, so it needs no translation. A translation that comes in with it (YouTube subtitles With translation, Translate to, or an answer column in a timed paste) goes into the card's notes as a line of its own, and any answer a video card still had is dropped. The card editor of a video card has no Answer box.
- **A question on its own stays where it is.** A card without a video time that has only a question (no answer) stays in whichever side it was made in. In Lessons it is studied with 🗣 Speaking like any other text card: it shows whole at once, with nothing to reveal and no answer lines, on dot paper instead of ruled paper (the same white sheet), and is graded Again or Good as usual. In Vocabulary it is a card to read: its flashcard is the back of a normal flashcard straight away, with nothing to turn: the card shows its sentence on a white sheet of dot paper instead of green, in the usual ink, with everything the back of a green flashcard has (no pinyin written over it, but pinyin or reading on pointing, other words of your lessons marked to look up, the stroke order, its notes, a click or two quick taps to edit it, a tap to play it on a phone), and no underline. Show (Space, Return) plays it; it is graded Again or Good like any other card, and ⇄ is hidden while such a card is showing, since there is no other side. Once its answer is filled in it is sorted again and moves by itself: a Lessons card whose new answer is in another language (a translation) goes to Vocabulary, and a Vocabulary word given a meaning in its own language goes to Lessons as a Speaking card (from the editor; in Lessons also from an import that fills in a missing answer, since Vocabulary's imports only add new words); ⌘Z undoes the move. Questions, words and notes have no length limit anywhere: a long term before `:` or `=` is still a word, a long bullet still becomes a card, and any selection on a PDF can be looked up.
- **Questions and answers without a video go to Vocabulary.** Lessons are for studying with YouTube: a pasted card whose answer translates its question (the two are in different languages) and that has no video time becomes a word and its meaning in Vocabulary instead of a card, also when made with Translate to. When a new lesson ends up with only words, it becomes a Vocabulary lesson and opens there. Cards with a video time, and questions answered in the same language (the cards studied with Speaking), stay in the lesson, and Speaking never asks to move a card. A lesson in Lessons that still holds translations without a video time (made before this rule, or typed in by hand) opens a large popup in the middle of the screen when you open it in Lessons, on a computer and on a phone: a green 字, how many cards belong in Vocabulary, and three buttons: **Move all** (Return) moves them all to Vocabulary at once, **Move this card** (only while one of them is the card being studied) moves just that one, and **Not now** (Esc, or a click outside) leaves them; ⌘Z undoes a move, and a lesson left with only words opens in Vocabulary. Not now is not asked again for that lesson until the app is opened again.
- Start a line with a timestamp (`1:23 question : answer`) and set the lesson's YouTube link to tie each card to a moment in the video.
- **The ＋ boxes show the card they make.** On the Add page and on a new lesson's page, where the lesson is not settled yet, the box for a single card starts as three plain boxes, Question, Answer and Notes, each with its coloured edge and nothing written in them, and takes the shape of its card as soon as you type. In a lesson's own ＋ popup it is in that lesson's shape from the start. Each shape has its hints in the boxes: in a video lesson it is YouTube red, with a time box in front of the sentence (filled in with where the video is when the box opens; type another time to change it; a sentence is not saved without one, since it would otherwise be a text card), Sentence and Notes, and no Answer box, since video cards have none; in a text lesson it is a sheet of ruled paper with its red margin line, with Question, Answer and Notes; a flashcard (in Vocabulary, or a translation typed in Lessons, which goes to Vocabulary) is in the lesson colour, with Question, Answer and Notes; in Vocabulary a question with nothing else is the white dot paper of the card to read it will become (in Lessons it stays the ruled sheet, as it stays a Speaking card). The boxes themselves stay plain and white. The paste box under it is tinted and outlined in the lesson's own colour (red in a video lesson, the lesson colour otherwise), and its Paste here! is written in that colour.
- **Choose where a paste goes.** Above the paste box, in Lessons and Vocabulary alike, on the Add page and on a new lesson's page, **Add to** picks the lesson the pasted cards or words go into: **🆕 New lesson** comes first and is outlined in the lesson colour while it is the choice (the box is exactly as wide as the browser itself needs for the choice it shows, so it is snug and never cuts off a letter, in Safari too) (a new, still empty lesson), followed by your lessons. Picking another lesson takes what you pasted along to it; picking 🆕 New lesson starts a new lesson with what you pasted.
- **The round ＋ in a lesson opens a large popup** (in ☰ Full and in Vocabulary's ☰ List, and ⌘⌥A): headed with just the lesson's name, with the same space above the name as below the last line (21 px on a computer, 17 px on a phone), it holds everything for adding to that lesson, the ＋ bar for a single card or word (clicking it opens the Question, Answer and Notes boxes, Word and Meaning in Vocabulary, right there in place of the bar; Save or Return adds the card and empties the boxes for the next one, which stay open until you click somewhere else or press Esc; a card that is already in the lesson is not added twice) and the paste box for many at once with its preview, delimiter, Import and Translate to, but not Add to or YouTube subtitles, since you are already in the lesson. Import closes it once something was added; ✕, Esc or a click outside closes it too. Under the paste box the lesson's total and its last additions are listed, in Lessons and Vocabulary alike: Total cards and each batch added (+2 cards on Oct 21 8:43 AM). Everything the app says calls Vocabulary's entries cards too, never words : its editor's boxes are Question and Answer, its empty page reads No cards yet, Reset and Delete count cards, and messages about adding speak of cards (Added 2 cards to Vocabulary, Added 1 card), and count in plain words, 1 card or 3 cards, 1 lesson or 4 lessons, never card(s).
- **One editor popup for both sides.** Editing a card in Vocabulary (E, a click on a row of ☰ List or on a flashcard) opens the same popup as Lessons, next to where you clicked: Question, Answer, Notes and Save, then the stroke order and the dictionary of the card's example sentence (of the card itself when it has no sentence). Save or a click outside keeps the changes, Esc throws them away.
- **One study order for both sides.** In Lessons and in Vocabulary alike, a study session takes the cards you missed first, then the ones you are learning (in a shuffled order), then the new ones from the top of the list to the bottom, in the list's own order; All lessons and All vocabularies stay random.
- **New things go on top.** Every way of adding, one at a time or pasted in bulk, puts the new cards at the top of the lesson and the new words at the top of the word list (a pasted batch keeps its own order). The ＋ on the Add cards page opens ☰ Full with the new card at the top, and the ＋ on Add vocabularies opens ☰ List with the new word at the top.
- Return saves in every box meant to save with it, also while typing with an input method (Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese…): the word box (`question: answer`), a card's question, answer and notes, a word's editor, the lesson's name and the one-line answers of a test. The Return that confirms what the input method was writing never adds a new line there; Shift+Return still starts a new line in notes.
- Each card has rich-text notes. Lines written as `question: answer` become words in the vocabulary deck. A word made from the card's own sentence (select part of the question, or of the answer in Speaking, and press Shift, in the editor or on the Speaking paper) first opens a small `question: answer` box, everywhere alike, its text exactly the size of the look-up popup's, since the two show the same `question: answer` (14 px on 20 px lines on a computer; both 16 px on 22 px on a phone, the smallest a phone types into without zooming in); nothing is added until you fill in the meaning and press Return (Esc closes it, so a stray Shift adds nothing). The word then goes into the notes (in the editor, into its Notes box, saved with the card) in the order the words come in the sentence: make the fourth word first and then the first, and the first goes above the fourth. Words that are not in the sentence (typed or pasted yourself) are left where they are, and a new word not in the sentence goes at the end. A word can be any length and may contain colons, so a whole sentence such as `Second: don't brush too hard.` moves to Vocabulary as it is, with its meaning.
- Questions, answers and notes keep only basic formatting: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, line breaks and bullet or numbered lists. Anything else pasted in (font sizes, colours, fonts, headings, links, images) is dropped, so a paste from a web page, Word or Google Docs cannot change how a card looks. In every editor, in Lessons and Vocabulary alike (question, answer and notes in the card editor, the word's notes, the quick add box), ⌘⇧7 (Ctrl+Shift+7) turns the selected lines into plain bullets and ⌘⇧8 (Ctrl+Shift+8) into bullets that are words: in a lesson's notes each becomes a word entry (**word**: meaning, the ": " added when the line has none), studied in Vocabulary; elsewhere ⌘⇧8 makes plain bullets too. Pressed on lines that already are bullets, either one turns them back into plain lines, whatever they hold, and so does Backspace at the very start of a bullet (a word entry turned back loses its bold and an empty ": ").
- Lessons can be saved as `.json` files and opened again, one at a time or all at once.

## Studying

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| ☰ Full | The whole lesson as a list, for browsing and editing. No spaced repetition. In the row of mode buttons it shows the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ (New, Due, Done) counts, like the study modes, each count lit up when it is not zero (hidden once everything is studied). The counts, here and in every mode on both sides, are only for looking at: clicking them does nothing, they cannot be selected as text (nor can the New, Due and Done totals and column heads in Statistics), and pointing at one turns the pointer into a question mark and says what it counts (New — never studied yet, Due — ready to review now, Done — 5 Good in a row). In a lesson made from subtitles only (no answers), the counts are those of its Listening cards, here, in the lesson list, in Statistics and in the calendar alike. A round ＋ button in the lesson colour floats at the bottom right of the list, over the cards as you scroll and just under the last one at the end of the list (on a phone, above the bottom bar, and higher while a card is selected): click it to add a card at the top of the list. It is always there in ☰ Full, and the list itself has no bar or Question / Answer header row. Vocabulary's ☰ List works the same way, its ＋ adding a word (All vocabularies has no ＋). Both lists are built alike: rows of the same height and type size, the text evenly spaced above and below, alternate rows lightly tinted in the lesson colour instead of grey (pale blue in Lessons, pale green in Vocabulary), lines between rows running edge to edge, and pointing at a row shades the whole row in the light lesson colour, the same single colour a selected row has (these two lists are the only places that shade on pointing). A new, empty lesson shows the add form with its own ＋, and a lesson whose last card is moved or deleted opens Add cards, with the lesson picked, as Vocabulary opens Add vocabularies when its last word goes. ☰ Full works exactly like Vocabulary's ☰ List: on a computer, click a card (its question or its answer) to open it in the editor, and point at it and press ⌘C (Ctrl+C on Windows) to copy its question or Delete to delete it; on a phone, tap a card once to select it and show the copy and ✕ bar, and tap it twice in quick succession to open the editor. Pointing at a card in ☰ Full of a video lesson shades the whole row, the same as pointing at a word in ☰ List, and a click anywhere on the row (or on a word's row) opens the editor; the text lesson's notebook page and every study mode shade nothing when you point at them. In Listening, pointing at the question, the answer or a line of notes shows that one's pinyin or reading, with no shading, and a click on the text opens the editor.  In a text lesson the list is a notebook page: white ruled paper with a red margin (on a phone there is no margin line, and the text sits a little further in from both edges), each question as a bold heading in the lesson colour with its answer written on the lines below, and the editor puts the short question on one line above a large answer box, the same size and not bold, without a dictionary. No table has a column of ✎, ✕ or 字 buttons, on a computer or a phone, in Lessons or in Vocabulary: point at a card (or select it on a phone) and press E to edit it, Delete to delete it or, in any lesson without a YouTube video, W to move it to Vocabulary, flying off as the logo (on a phone the bar of copy, ✕ and 字 described below does the same): the question becomes a word, the answer its meaning, and words filed under the card go along; if the sentence is already a word, the card just leaves the lesson. Undo brings it back. In every editor a coloured line starts each box: blue for the question, green for the answer, grey for the notes, and the answer box fits its text. Only the notes box is named (Notes); the question and answer boxes carry no Question or Answer label, their colour line telling them apart. Every editor writes the question in plain (not bold) type, the same size as the answer; outside the editors, the notebook and Speaking paper questions and the words in Vocabulary's ☰ List stay bold. |
| Listening | Laid out like YouTube, video and sentence in one single white frame (one border, one shadow, rounded only at its outer corners): on a computer the video on the left and, on the right, the sentence being studied, its side exactly as tall as the video and its bar (its height worked out in whole pixels). Under the video, as in ☰ Full, sits its bar, with no name on it: only the time played, a seek bar and the video's length on one line, 16 px under the video and lined up with its left and right edges, so the video and its bar make one clear block to line the rest up against (it stays under the video on a phone and in All lessons too; dragging the seek bar stops the clip that is looping, as in ☰ Full); on a phone the video on top and the sentence under it, the frame filling the screen down to the bars at the bottom (see **One card, the whole height** below). There is no list of the other sentences: one sentence at a time, the next one coming up once it is graded (New, Due and Done as everywhere). Everything stays pinned: on a phone the page itself never scrolls in Listening, and the video sits in the layout itself (it never trails behind or jumps when the phone's browser moves the page). The sentence is set as large as a flashcard, with the same room around it: 28 px on 40 px lines with 25 px above and below and 30 px at the sides (24 px on 35 px lines and 20 px all round on a phone), in the same plain (not bold) type. The answer and a plain line of notes, both in green, are 4 px smaller than the sentence: 24 px on 34 px lines (20 px on 30 px lines on a phone); a flashcard uses just two sizes: its main line (the word on the front, and on the back the sentence it is in, or the word itself when the sentence does not hold it) at 28 px on 35 px lines, and everything else on it (the sentence under the word, the green meaning, the word's own notes) a touch smaller at 26 px on 32 px lines, so it stays easy to read; on a phone 24 px on 30 px and 22 px on 28 px. Just these two sizes, on a computer and a phone alike. Whatever shows on the front is the size it has on the back: the word at the main size, and, when ⇄ shows the meaning first, the meaning at the meaning's size. There is no frame or box: the video floats on the page with 20 px corners and a soft shadow in the lesson colour, over a gentle glow of the lesson colour that fades out well before the page's edges (like YouTube's ambient light), and beside it (under it on a phone) the sentence stands straight on the page, left-aligned, 25 px from the video; on a computer, where it sits beside the video, its whole block (question, answer and notes) is centred between the video's top edge and the bottom of its bar as the eye sees it, from the top of the first line's capitals to the bottom of the last line or chip (the room a line keeps above its letters is matched under the chips), and before it is shown the question's line already sits where it will be once everything appears (the room of the answer and notes is kept, invisible); where it sits under the video (on a phone and in narrow windows) its time badge starts 16 px under the video's bar, and on a phone 20 px from both sides. There is no extra space between the parts, on a computer and a phone alike: the answer simply starts on the next line under the question, and the notes' chips 8 px under the answer and 8 px under a plain line of notes, the same as between two rows of chips, however the notes were typed or pasted (text written straight before a list counts as its own line): the time badge in front of the question, then the answer in green, then the card's notes as small rounded chips side by side (15 px on 20 px lines, white with a thin border), one chip per word. Until it is shown there is only the question's line: its time badge and three big dots (···), exactly where the question will be once shown, and the notes simply appear under it; The frame never grows: if the sentence and its notes do not fit, the text scrolls inside it (mouse wheel, trackpad or a swipe of the finger), and the page does not; on a computer the scrolling text keeps its 25 px margin, disappearing 25 px inside the video's top and bottom edges rather than at them. Click or tap the time badge to play the clip over and over (again to stop). Until the sentence being studied is shown, the video keeps playing but heavily blurred under a light dark veil, with a white sound wave and the clip's time over it, so you still see its colours and movement while its burnt-in subtitles can never be read; the bars sway slowly and gently only while the clip is playing and stand still when it is not, and clicking or tapping the video plays or stops the sentence being studied, before and after it is shown, exactly like its time badge (the video itself never takes the click, so YouTube's own pause and controls never get in the way). Once the sentence is shown the cover fades away quickly and the video comes into focus in under half a second, shrinking back to its size around its own centre (it never slides to a corner), and plays sharp in the frame. That is the only moment it animates, and switching to Listening from another mode never animates anything: when the video finishes loading, when the next sentence covers it again or when anything else changes, the cover simply is or is not there (a lesson with no video keeps the cover). The sound follows the same rules as before: the clip plays by itself when a card comes up; Space shows the card and from then on its clip plays over and over (also when Space is pressed while the clip is still playing by itself: it finishes that pass and then keeps looping), or it is read aloud over and over when it has none, until you stop it with Space or the sound button. Editing (E, or two quick taps on a phone) opens the card editor as a popup next to where you clicked, the sentence staying where it is behind it: Question (blue), Answer (green) and Notes (Notes is set apart like a note clipped to the card: a light lesson-coloured sheet with a short paper clip in the lesson colour, tilted outwards, clipped over its top left corner, its top level with the ring the box gets when clicked; only the first line moves over to make room for it, starting at the same place whether it is text or a bullet, while the bullets below start 18 px in from the box's edge and the lines of text below 16 px, so it never touches the text, a bullet or the placeholder, and the lines below start at the same left edge as the boxes above; emptied, it shrinks back to one line with its placeholder), Save, then the sentence's stroke order and its dictionary at its full size, spaced exactly like the card editor in ☰ Full (10 px between parts, 10 px top and bottom, 12 px each side); when it all does not fit, the popup scrolls (the dictionary never shrinks; it sits inside the popup itself, so it scrolls smoothly with everything else instead of trailing behind, and a swipe on it scrolls the popup once the dictionary's own page is at its end). On a computer with a window at least 1000 px wide, the popup opens wide instead, as one box split in two: the dictionary fills its left half from top to bottom, edge to edge, and the editor (Question, Answer, Notes, Save and the stroke order) its right half, divided by one thin line; both halves are exactly as tall as each other (640 px, less in a short window), the editor scrolling inside its half when it holds more, so you read the dictionary while you edit. Like every dictionary in the app it is loaded ahead of time: the current card's and the next cards' dictionaries already wait, loaded, inside the hidden popup, so opening the editor just shows it. Save or a click outside keeps the changes, Esc throws them away, and while it is open the study keys do nothing. ## Tests

📝 Tests sits in the logo row, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike (in a phone's top bar too), counting the tests in progress and the ones due, and lit while the test centre is open. ⌘⌥X (Ctrl+Alt+X) opens it too. Tests are built for active recall: every answer is brought back from memory and typed or spoken, never picked. There is no multiple choice, no word bank, no scrambled words to put in order and no list to choose from, and each word is tested only once on a paper. A word used in one question never comes back in another part further down. When a paper needs more words than there are, it has fewer questions instead.

**Languages first.** The test centre opens on four big language cards: English, 한국어, 中文 and 日本語, each in its own colours with a large letter (Aa, 한, 中, 日), its number of words and how far its route has gone. A language opens once it has at least 3 of your Vocabulary words; until then its card is greyed and dashed. Clicking the language you are already on, or 📝 while the test centre is open, changes nothing, and the page only plays its short entrance (the banner and the route's cards rising in) when it opens or another language is picked, not on every button. Each language is its own world and never mixes with another: its papers use only its words, and everything in it is written in that language (路线, 第1步, 开始… for Chinese), except the ✦ Claude key at the top.

**The route.** Under the cards, a large banner in the language's colours names the next step of its route with a ring showing the steps passed, your words, tests, average and best score. Its buttons are:

- ▶ **Start step N** (or Resume, while that step's paper is in progress).
- 🎯 **Weak cards**: a test of the cards you missed lately and the ones with the most lapses.
- ⚡ **Quick check**: a short test of missing words and translations.
- ✎ **Build a test**: opens the builder below.

The route itself is a path of seven steps, each with its parts, number of questions, length, best score, tries, and Start, Retake and Review buttons:

1. **Recall**: dictation, missing words and translation.
2. **Use**: your own sentences, speaking and writing.
3. **Vocabulary mastery**: every vocabulary part on one paper.
4. **Understanding**: the real exam's reading, listening and word parts.
5. **Expression**: the real exam's writing and speaking parts.
6. **Mock exam**: the whole exam at standard length.
7. **Final exam**: the long version, timed like the real day.

Steps 1–3 are Vocabulary tests; steps 4–7 use the language's real exam (IELTS for English, TOPIK for Korean, HSK for Chinese, JLPT for Japanese). A step opens when the step before reaches 70 %. A step whose parts all need Claude shows ✦ Needs a Claude key and is skipped until a key is added. A finished step's score report has ▶ Next: step N to go straight on.

The rest of the language's page:

- **Waiting for you**: the tests in progress and scheduled, with Resume or Start and ✕.
- **Results**: every finished test in that language with a coloured score medal, its band and date, Review, ↻ Retake and ✕, the last five first (Show all for the rest), and a small chart of the scores.
- **Skills**: bars per skill from the last ten tests.
- **Cards to work on**: the cards you missed, with 🎯 Test these cards.

**Build your own test.** ✎ Build a test opens a builder in the language's colours. Choose the exam (Vocabulary test, or the language's exams), the words (All vocabularies or one lesson's) and the parts, which can be switched on and off. Then choose the length (Short, Standard or Long) and, optionally, a date (Take it on). Finish with ▶ **Start now** or 📅 **Save for later**; a saved test waits as Scheduled and is marked due from that day. Each exam keeps only the question types that make you think, laid out and worded like the real one:

**The exam system.** ✕, then Delete?, removes a test. A finished test opens on its score report, a printed sheet in the same style as the paper and in its language (成绩单, SCORE REPORT…). It shows:

- the PHILLIP ACADEMY heading, the exam's name, the words and the date of the test;
- the candidate lines and the paper code;
- a ruled table with a row per part (number, part, score, out of, %) and a bold total row;
- the result (★ Excellent from 85 %, Good from 70 %, Pass from 50 %, otherwise Needs work), the time used and how many answers were fully right, beside the red score stamp.

Once the paper is marked (straight after Hand in, or after your last own mark), the page goes back to the top, to this sheet. Under the sheet, side by side, are cards that each say what they do, in the paper's language: ← Test centre (back to your route and all your results), 🎯 Practise missed cards (a short new test of the words this paper got wrong, with how many), ↻ Retake (the same exam again, with new questions) and, on a route step passed at 70 % or more, ▶ Next: step N with the step's name (the next step of your route), the one to take next filled in colour. The marked paper follows.

A test is saved as you go, every answer as you type it. Close the app, reload or come back days later, and Resume opens the paper exactly where you left it, with your typed answers and the time left. Pause on the paper does the same at once. The clock only runs while the paper is open and stops while answers are being marked.

Tests are part of the backup (💾) and come back with it. A test keeps its own copy of the words and sentences it uses (only those, once the paper is written, so tests stay small). It therefore does not depend on your lessons: deleting a lesson or a word leaves its tests, papers and scores untouched (only Retake needs the words to still be there). Test data goes away only when you delete a test, when a finished test falls beyond the 50 most recent ones, or when the browser's site data is cleared; restoring a backup brings tests back.

**The paper.** It is laid out like a real printed exam paper: plain white sheets in a serif type, headed on the left by PHILLIP ACADEMY with how many pages it has right under it (This paper has 01 page), and on the right by the exam's name in capitals, the words it uses and the time allowed; under them the candidate name and number lines on dotted rules and a boxed paper code; each part opens with one bold line (PART I – Listening, 第一部分, 一、听力…) followed by its question numbers and instructions; questions are numbered the exam's way (Câu 1., 1.), reading texts and scripts sit in a thin box, blanks are dotted lines, answers are written in blue on ruled lines, and each sheet ends with its page and paper code (Trang 1/4 - Mã đề thi 0115). A sheet holds two parts. The clock and Pause sit outside the paper, above it, and stay in view as you scroll. Recordings and questions to hear are a plain round ▶ button with two dots showing the plays left (●● then ●○): while the recording loads the button spins, and a play is only counted once the sound actually starts (it turns into ■, which stops it); if it does not start within 15 seconds, or YouTube cannot play it, nothing is counted and ▶ can be pressed again; speaking is a round ● that turns into a red ■ while listening. Once marked, the score is stamped in red in a double circle beside the examiner's handwritten-style comment (Lời phê), and each answer gets a red ✓, ✗ or score in the margin. Nothing is written twice: a fully right answer keeps only its ✓; otherwise one red line under your answer gives the right version, merged with yours: what you wrote wrong or added is struck through in grey, and what should be there, including what you left out, follows it underlined in bold (words, or characters in Chinese and Japanese), as in began ~~abduction~~ __abducting__ or 银行~~子~~, as a teacher marks a paper (capitals, spaces and punctuation are not counted as mistakes), followed by a short comment when there is one. A dictation shows its meaning under ▶ once marked, and the sentence itself only in that red line when your answer was not right. In the dark theme the sheets stay light, a little dimmer than white. Each paper speaks its exam's language, as the real one does: everything printed on it (the Candidate, Date and Marked by lines, the instructions box, Score and Examiner's comments, the part instructions and their numbering, the buttons to play a recording, speak and hand in, the end line and the examiner's comments, and the score report above a marked paper; and the test's row in the test centre and its exam card) is in Chinese on an HSK paper, Japanese on JLPT, Korean on TOPIK and English on IELTS and SAT, and a Vocabulary test is written in the language of its words; Claude's comments on each answer are in that language too. Only the Vocabulary test adds help in your own language, since translating from it is part of the test (the sentence's meaning under a missing word, the meanings of the words to use, comments in your language); a real-exam paper has none.

**Listening from your videos.** A dictation question first looks for a word whose saved sentence comes from a YouTube lesson: the ▶ then plays that very stretch of the video (from the sentence's time to the next one's), unseen, so you hear the real voice, and the sentence is what you write; only when no such sentence exists is a sentence read aloud by the browser (or, with a Claude key, a new one written and read aloud). Listening passages written by Claude are read aloud.

**Speaking.** A speaking question is answered out loud: ▶ Hear the question reads it aloud, ● Speak starts listening (the browser asks once for the microphone) and ■ Stop ends it; what you said is written on the ruled lines as you speak, and ● Speak again replaces it. The written-down words are what is marked, out of 4 (answering the question, using the given words, grammar, range of words), with spelling and punctuation ignored since they come from speech recognition. JLPT and SAT have no speaking part, as in the real exams. Where the browser cannot listen (Firefox, for one) or listening fails, the answer is typed instead.

**How a test runs.** The whole paper is written as soon as it is opened and shown at once, every part on its own sheet, as on a printed exam: answer the questions in any order, go back to any of them, and press **Hand in** at the end of the last sheet when you are done (with unanswered questions it first asks Hand in with 3 unanswered?, and a second press hands in). The words you find hardest (most lapses, never studied) are used first, each word once on the whole paper. Return moves to the next answer box (in longer answers it starts a new line). Recordings play at most twice. The time allowed adds up each question's time (for example 1 minute to listen and write, 45 seconds for a missing word, 2 minutes per reading question, 8–40 minutes for writing, by exam); the clock turns red in the last minute, and when it runs out the paper is handed in as it stands. Everything is marked after handing in; the last question of each missing-word, translation or correction part also asks you to explain why, and a wrong or missing reason halves its mark.

**Marking.** A Claude API key is a personal password for the Anthropic API, starting with sk-ant- and made at console.anthropic.com (the form links there); Anthropic charges a little for each paper written and marked, and a Claude Pro subscription does not include one. With a key (added with ✦ Add a Claude key at the top of the test centre, kept only in this browser and never offered to the browser's password or address autofill, so nothing is filled into the lesson search; Remove key takes it out), every part is offered: Claude writes brand-new questions in the exam's style for each paper and marks each answer by its meaning against a rubric, accepting every correct alternative, not only the model answer (1 point, ½ for a small slip, 0; Writing and Speaking out of 4). Without a key, the parts that need Claude (reading and listening passages, readings, corrections, rewriting) are greyed out; the rest use your saved sentences, Listening and missing words are marked by how close your answer is, a translation, correction or rewrite almost the same as the model answer (one small slip) gets ½ by itself, and only the other open answers, which may be right in other words, are marked by you. While some are left, the bar pinned above the paper says how many (2 left to mark · Next ↓) and takes you to the next one; each mark moves the page on to the next answer to mark, and the last one brings you up to the score sheet. Under each one a MARK IT YOURSELF box, in the paper's language, shows the model answer with the differences from yours marked as in a correction (your words struck through, the model's underlined), one line on what counts, and the buttons ✓ Right, ½ Nearly and ✗ Wrong (4 to 0 for Writing and Speaking). An answer you mark right keeps only its ✓. If Claude cannot be reached during a test, the paper carries on this way.

A word is looked up only where the text holds exactly that word, ignoring case, spaces and punctuation (so 送货 is found in 开车送货 and `cat.` in "a cat sat"); other forms of a word (went for go, 食べました for 食べる, 먹어요 for 먹다) are not matched, and a word is never split into its single characters to look them up one by one. Words are found by the kind of writing at their edges, with no language setting and no lists of words to skip:

- Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese, written without spaces, match anywhere.
- Korean must start a spaced chunk but may end inside it, so 학교 matches 학교에서 but not 대학교.
- Languages written with spaces (English, Vietnamese, Russian…) must match whole words, so "cat" does not match "concatenate".
- Full-width and half-width forms count as the same (ｶﾀｶﾅ and カタカナ, ＡＢＣ and ABC), katakana and hiragana count as the same (ネコ and ねこ), Vietnamese tone marks may sit on either vowel (hòa and hoà, khỏe and khoẻ, thúy and thuý), case does not matter, and line breaks count as spaces.

## Reading PDFs

A lesson can hold a PDF to read beside its video, in Lessons. The button at the start of the link box, inside it (a small page with a red PDF band), adds a PDF from your computer or phone; a PDF link pasted in the box and opened with Open is added the same way.

**Links.** The link box holds the lesson's video (YouTube, TikTok, Douyin) and opens PDFs; any other link (an article, a NotebookLM notebook, a page you read) pasted in it and opened with Open joins the lesson's **list of links** right under the box, newest last, and opens in a new tab, while the box keeps the video. The links hang together under the link bar as one cluster, as wide as the bar, 4 px apart, and sway very gently. Once there are links the bar has a hole punched near each end of its bottom edge (in the link box on the left, in Clear on the right), and every link a hole near each end of its top edge; two cords run from the bar's holes down over each link's top edge into its holes, behind the link and across the gap to the next one, ending tied in the last link's holes. With no links, nothing is punched. Each link is a slim row with a link icon, the page's title when it is known and its address: click it to open it, ✕ to remove it (Undo brings it back). When the browser extension sends words into a lesson, the page they were read on is added to that lesson's list by itself, once, with its title. A lesson that used to keep a page link in the box has it moved to the list. The same box, PDF reader and list are on the lesson's Vocabulary page too, in Vocabulary's green, so nothing of the lesson is missing there; in All lessons and All vocabularies there is no box. Either way the lesson's video link stays where it is: the PDF comes on top of it, never instead of it. Open and Clear sit 8 px apart, the same 8 px the box keeps from Open, and Clear clears the video link only.

The PDF opens under the link box in a reader of its own: a plain toolbar with the file's name on the left and, on the right, the page you are on (3 / 12), zoom (− 100% +, from 50% to 200%) and ✕, which takes the PDF out of the lesson, in groups split by thin lines, every button the same 32 px; then the pages, on the same white as the rest of the app, each outlined with a soft shadow and its number under it, scrolling inside the reader (640 px tall on a computer, 480 px on a phone) and drawn only as you come near them, so long books open quickly. Words already in your lessons are highlighted in its text in the same green as in the cards; resting on one shows the same lookup as the browser extension: the word in bold, its pinyin on a line of its own for Chinese, its meaning under it when it has one (a card with only a question shows none, rather than a dash), and the lesson it is in, small and in italics. A lookup too long for the screen (a card with many notes, say) opens above or below the word, wherever there is more room, no taller than that room, and scrolls inside itself; scrolling it never moves the page or closes it. Wherever a lookup names the lesson a word or sentence comes from (the word popup in the cards, when the sentence is from another lesson, and the note on the reading card), the lesson's name is in italics and muted, never bold.

**Picking words, as in the browser extension.** Select a word or phrase and the green 字 button appears beside it (Shift opens the card straight away). The card is the extension's: the word (a long phrase wraps onto more lines, under the buttons, instead of being cut off), 🔊 to hear it and a button to translate the whole sentence, the three buttons evenly spaced in the top right corner, the meaning translated for you beside a green bar (a long one wraps too) with other meanings to pick from, the sentence it came from on a light green ground with the word marked (click it to edit), a note when the word is already in one of your lessons, and Add (or Enter). The word goes into this lesson's words with its sentence, and shows in Vocabulary with it; the PDF's name is not added. On a phone, the 字 button appears once the selection has stayed still a moment. Scrolling the PDF or the page closes the card; when no translation can be had (offline, say), the meaning box simply asks you to type it.

The PDF itself stays in this browser only: the lesson (its .json file and the cloud copy) keeps just its name and link, so it stays small. A linked PDF opens again by itself on any device; a file opened from your computer opens again on this one, and on another device the reader asks you to add the file once more. Some sites do not let other apps read their PDFs; then the reader says so, and you can download the PDF and add the file instead. In the study modes the reader is hidden with the link box. All lessons, All vocabularies and Vocabulary have no reader: the words you add show in Vocabulary as usual.

- A dictionary in the card editor (and, outside the editor, at the foot of the Speaking paper; Listening and the flashcards have it only in the editor), in every language: chinesedictionary.mobi for Chinese; Bing Translator into English for Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese, and into Vietnamese for English.
- Reading aloud with the browser's own voices
- Study languages: Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Japanese, Korean, English, Vietnamese

## Progress and sync

- **Stats** shows your daily streak, a review heatmap and progress per lesson. The heatmap shows six months at a time; ‹ and › move back and forward and ○ returns to today, and each appears only when there is somewhere to go. Click a day to list its cards just under the heatmap, grouped by lesson: on a past day the cards and words you studied (with ✓ or ✕ for how you last answered, and 字 on words), on a coming day the cards due then. Click a card to open its lesson (in Vocabulary for a word); click the day again to close the list. On a phone the heatmap opens scrolled to the latest months, each card in the day's list shows its answer on a second line, and the table of lessons keeps its names readable and scrolls sideways when it is wider than the screen.
- Everything is stored in the browser; no account is needed.
- Sign in with Google or email to sync lessons and reviews across devices through Firebase.
- Light and dark themes.

## Libraries

Loaded from CDNs at runtime: [pinyin-pro](https://github.com/zh-lx/pinyin-pro), [Hanzi Writer](https://hanziwriter.org), the YouTube IFrame API, and the Firebase SDK (only when you sign in).
