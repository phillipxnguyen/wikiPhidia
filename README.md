# wikiPhidia

Flashcards for learning Chinese and Japanese, with spaced repetition, YouTube clips, dictionaries and a vocabulary deck built from your notes.

The app is called **Flashcard**, and its icon is a white 字 on a blue rounded square, the same as the mark in its top bar: it shows in the browser tab and on bookmarks, and becomes the app's name and icon on a phone's home screen (Share → Add to Home Screen on an iPhone, Install app on Android), where it opens on its own, without the browser's bars. The tab reads the lesson's name followed by · Flashcard.

The whole app is one file, `index.html`. There is no build step and nothing to install: open it in a browser, or host it anywhere that serves static files. On a phone it can be added to the home screen and runs full screen.

## Lessons and Vocabulary

**Lessons and Vocabulary, two sides again.** Clicking the 字 logo switches between Lessons (blue) and Vocabulary (green), each with its own list of lessons in the sidebar, exactly as before 2.0: Lessons studies the sentences, Vocabulary the words. A lesson's page is its list (☰ Full), and a YouTube lesson's page is the list under its large video, as before 2.0. The study modes are started from the round buttons stacked at the bottom right, above ≡ Words and ＋ (the toolbar has no mode buttons any more), all the same size and 16 px apart, like ≡ and ＋, and never above the toolbar: they rest at the bottom of the screen and, when there is not room for all of them there, stay 16 px under the toolbar instead of covering it; each is shown only where it can be used: in Lessons, the speech bubble for Speaking and the screen for the theater (YouTube lessons); Podcast, made for learning words, lives only in Vocabulary; in Vocabulary, the card for the flashcards and the headphones for Podcast. There is no 字 word button inside a lesson or on the theater any more: words are studied on the Vocabulary side. In a task, a word comes either as its flashcard or in Podcast, at random, as the other cards come in their own modes. Every mode keeps its own way of studying: you see or hear, recall it yourself, Show, and grade it; no exercises to type into or answers to pick. Every card and word is edited in the same popup, everywhere (the list, a study mode, the theater). Podcast studies every word the same way: a moving picture while the word itself is read aloud (R or the sound button reads it again), then Show brings out the word, its meaning and its sentences, which are played one after another, then Good or Again; Delete (on a phone the ✕ of the selection bar) drops a word you do not want to learn for good (a word of your notes is deleted and also hidden, so the lesson does not find it again by itself); ⌘Z brings it back.

The list itself (☰ Full) is where you read, look up and edit. A lesson from YouTube shows its theater instead (see **The theater**). Today, its tasks, the daily limits and everything you have studied stay as they were.

## Search or Ask

The box at the top of the sidebar (**Search or Ask**) opens one search for everything, like Spotlight on a Mac: click it, or press **⌘/** (Ctrl / on Windows, the **/ ?** key) or **/** anywhere. A large rounded field drops in high on the screen, like Spotlight, frosted over the page, with the results in a second frosted panel under it. Type part of a word you half remember and it finds it even with a letter wrong or missing (*scren* finds *screen*), with or without accents (*kiem tra* finds *kiểm tra*), in any of your lessons:

- **Words**: each word with its meaning (in the answers' green) and its lesson; Enter (or a click) opens it in its lesson's Vocabulary list, in its editor.
- **Cards**: the card's question with its answer and lesson; Enter opens the lesson in ☰ Full at that card, in its editor.
- **Lessons** by name; Enter opens the lesson.
- **Recent**: with nothing typed, the list shows only what you opened lately: the words, cards, lessons and look-ups you picked in Search, newest first, then the lessons you opened last, up to 8 in all (kept in this browser only). There are no buttons for other parts of the app here; they are in the sidebar. A long list scrolls inside its panel.
- **Look up "…"**: always last, opens the dictionary for what you typed in a new tab, for a word that is in none of your lessons yet.

↑ ↓ move through the results (the one picked is tinted, with ↵ at its end), Enter opens it, Esc or a click outside closes it, and ⌘/ again too. On a phone the field sits at the top of the screen; open it from the box in the menu.

## Lessons and cards

- **One name.** The app is called **Flashcard** beside its 字 logo, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike (only its colour changes, blue or green); clicking it switches between the two.
- **Every box you type in is centred.** The writing sits in the middle of its box, with the same room above and below (the Notes box too, now exactly as tall as the card box, with a list in it or not), and the grey hint shown in an empty box (Notes, Untitled lesson, No limit, Email…) is the same size as the writing and the same grey everywhere, written in a bold dot-matrix pixel font (Doto, built into the app so it works offline), as is the sidebar's Search or Ask box; hints end without “…”, and on a phone the link box just says “Paste a link”.
- **Big writing in every box you type cards into.** The card editor (Question, Answer, Notes), the ＋ boxes for a single card or word, Vocabulary's editor and the small box for a new word follow the same sizes as the cards outside them: every box a card is typed in, question, answer (word and meaning) and notes alike, is the same: 22 px on 30 px lines and 46 px tall (20 px on 28 px lines and 43 px tall on a phone), in the card editor, the ＋ boxes and Vocabulary's editor; how a card looks outside its boxes is another matter; lists in them are indented to match. The paste box (Paste here!) is not a card and keeps its usual size, like the other boxes around it: 14 px on 20 px lines (16 px on 24 px on a phone). Every list follows the Speaking paper: the question and the answer are the same size, 20 px, and the notes a little smaller, 20 px on 28 px lines. So in ☰ Full the sentence of a video lesson is 20 px with its notes at 18 px, as the question, answer and notes of a text lesson's notebook already were, and in Vocabulary's ☰ List the word and its meaning are 20 px while the notes under the meaning, in the same column, are 18 px. On a phone everything is one step smaller in the same proportion: the question and answer 20 px on 28 px lines and the notes 18 px on 25 px, in the editors, the lists, the notebook page and the Speaking paper (whose ruled lines are 32 px apart there), and the notebook page keeps the same room on both sides of the writing (about 25 px from the paper's edge); in ☰ Full the time badge and the sentence start 17 px in from the card's left edge (21 px on a computer), the notes' bullets start 6 px inside the sentence's left edge, while on the right the notes stop where the sentence stops (17 px before the card's edge on a phone, 21 px on a computer); the bullets of the notebook page and of Vocabulary's ☰ List sit 6 px in from their text the same way, their right edge again that of the text, and a plain line of notes starts in line with the bullets, and Vocabulary's ☰ List rows keep 16 px on both sides. In Vocabulary's ☰ List the word's column takes up to two thirds of the row when its words are long (a whole sentence, say), and only as much as its words need otherwise, so a long sentence with a short meaning no longer squeezes into a narrow column beside an empty one. Boxes that are already large, such as the search box, the lesson's name and the link box, stay as they are.
- **The flag.** In front of the lesson's name, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike, its left edge in line with the link box and the cards below and centred on the name's line, a real flag (from the flag-icons set, built into the app) waves on a small pole with a gold knob (every flag in the app, here and on the toolbars, is the same 32 × 24 px cloth, the flags' own 4 : 3 shape so the picture fills it edge to edge, sized to sit well beside the 36 px sound button; several languages hang together from that one pole: a rope leaves its top nearly level, and each flag, as big as a lone one (32 × 24 px), upright and 4 px from the next (with four or five flags they overlap a little, starting 22 px apart, so the rope stays short), is tied to it at a point of its left edge picked at random and sways about it on its own, free to wave over whatever is around it: the pole keeps its place and the name stays where it always is, the rope simply running out over it, the main language nearest the pole; while you edit the name it comes up in front of the flags, on its white box, so you see what you type): the whole cloth swings and a soft fold of light runs across it, so it never breaks into strips. Click it to pick the language (Auto-detect, Simplified, Traditional, Korean, Japanese, English or Vietnamese). With Auto-detect it shows the language most of the lesson's cards are in, and while a card is being studied the language of that card; where there is no single language (an empty lesson, or a lesson with several languages) it shows several smaller flags on their own poles waving side by side, one per language (up to five; 🇬🇧 🇨🇳 🇰🇷 🇯🇵 🇻🇳 when nothing tells). There is no flag behind the page: every page keeps the plain page colour. With reduced motion turned on the flag does not wave.
- **Clean spacing everywhere.** One shared cleaner tidies every text that comes in or is saved: pasted and imported cards, translations, YouTube subtitles, lesson files and backups, the card editor, words and meanings, lesson names. Words are never separated by more than one space (tabs, non-breaking and wide spaces count as spaces too), lines start and end with no space, and Chinese and Japanese characters have no space between them (今天  我们 去 becomes 今天我们去), while Korean keeps its single spaces between words. Lessons saved before are tidied the same way when they are opened.
- Paste cards into a lesson, one per line, as `question : answer` or tab-separated. The separator is detected and a preview shows what will be imported. Column delimiter offers Auto-detect, Tab and Colon ( : ), in Lessons and Vocabulary alike; only Tab also takes a time column and a notes column. **Auto-detect**, the first choice and the default on both sides, reads a whole study text in one paste, in Lessons or in Vocabulary alike: a question line just above a paragraph (ending in ?, starting with Q: or Question:, or a short heading such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes one card with the whole paragraph as its answer (an A: or Answer: in front is dropped; following paragraphs belong to the same answer), any other paragraph becomes one question-only card per sentence, each `question = answer` or `question: answer` line (with or without a `*`, `-`, `•` or `1.` in front) becomes a word, a line with Tab columns is read column by column as under Tab (time, question, answer, notes; it is never taken as the lesson title), and a short line just above a word list (such as `🔥 Phrasal verbs to conquer today`) is left out. Each word is filed under the card whose question or answer uses it, written exactly the same (case, spaces and punctuation aside), so clicking it there shows that sentence, and the back of its flashcard shows the sentence with that form highlighted (the back always uses a sentence of the lesson that contains the word, even if the word was filed under another card); a word no sentence uses goes to the lesson's own word list. A short first line without sentence punctuation (such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes the lesson title when the lesson is empty, and stays the question of its card when a paragraph follows it. A dialogue (lines such as `Patient: …` and `Dentist: …`, where the same speakers come back) is kept as it is under Auto-detect: each line is one card with its speaker, never split at the colon, and a line ending in ? is not taken as the question of the line under it. From Auto-detect, pasted cards or words still switch to Tab or colon by themselves when more than half the lines use it and no line holds several sentences (a colon after a finished sentence does not count, nor do Q:, A:, Question: and Answer: labels, so a paste of short Q: and A: lines stays questions and answers); from any other choice, Auto-detect is picked when no line has a Tab or colon. **One box for the card, then the notes.** Every place you write a card or a word has one box only, plus Notes: the card editor, the quick add (＋) in Lessons and Vocabulary, and the word editor in Vocabulary. A first line starting with `/` is the question (or the word) and the lines under it are its answer (or meaning); a plain sentence needs no `/`. The `/` writes itself: as soon as the box has a second line (Shift+Return, or a paste of several lines) and the first line has no `/`, one is put in front of it, so the first line is the question and the rest its answer; a single line stays a plain sentence. A video sentence's box is left as it is. There is no separate Answer or Meaning box anywhere. Nothing is studied as a translation: when you want one, write it yourself as the answer under `/`, and a paste with a translation is read the same way (`sentence : translation`). Everything is shown as before: Speaking asks the question, then Hint (when the answer has words with meanings) and Show the answer, with its notes beside it. A sentence card on its own (no `/` question) gets a front built from it on each review, with nothing written around it: the sentence with one word cut out as a blank line the length of the word (a word of its notes when it has one, otherwise one of its longer words), or only that word, large, to recall the whole sentence from; Show brings out the sentence and its notes. A word's flashcard picks one of three fronts at random each time, all in the studied language only: the word alone (say a sentence with it yourself, then Show brings out the lesson's sentences), its own sentence with the word cut out, or a sentence from elsewhere that uses the word, cut out the same way: from another of your lessons, or from the world, real sentences people wrote on Tatoeba (tatoeba.org) and in Wikipedia articles in the studied language. For Chinese they always come in the lesson's own script, never mixed: a lesson in Simplified gets every sentence in Simplified and one in Traditional in Traditional (a sentence found in the other script is converted first, and Wikipedia is asked in the right variant). Those are asked for online, word by word, only when the word comes up for study (and for the next dozen words of a session). The more contexts the better: every time a word comes back it gets a world sentence it has not shown before, and when only a few unseen ones are left the next page is fetched from Tatoeba and Wikipedia, so a word can go through hundreds of different sentences over time; about 40 unseen sentences per word are kept in this browser, so nothing is downloaded in bulk and the app stays light. A world sentence is the front of a word's flashcard more often than the other fronts whenever one is available; offline, or when nothing is found, the lesson's own sentences are used as before. A sentence is taken only when the word stands as a word of its own in it (快乐 does not count for 乐), never cut short, and never one already in the lesson. Podcast also reads out two such sentences (different each time) among the lesson's, shown with the others on Show, and the dictionary in the card editor shows two of them under each new word, In the world. Sentences from Tatoeba and Wikipedia are under CC BY licences. A sentence is always shown whole, never cut short with …, so the word keeps its full context. A front with a blank is not turned over: Show keeps the same card, fills the blank with the word (marked) and adds only what was missing under it, the meaning and the word's notes. Nothing moves on Show: the blank is already as wide as the word and the meaning's place is kept from the start, only unseen, so they simply appear where they already were. A paste is never turned into translated Vocabulary sentences. The Chinese script buttons (Simplified, Traditional) convert a pasted Chinese text before it is imported; nothing is translated. Bullets in front of a line are dropped with every delimiter. A time in front of a line becomes the card's video time with every delimiter, also in brackets (`[4:53]` or `(4:53)`) and also when it stands on a line of its own above its sentence, as in a transcript copied from YouTube (`4:53` on one line, the sentence on the next). A lesson made earlier whose cards still carry such times at the start of their text (most of its cards, at least three) gets them back as video times when it is opened: the time leaves the question (its formatting kept), and the answer too when it starts with the same time; those cards keep their progress and are not merged.
- **Sentences come in threes.** Short subtitle lines are joined into longer stretches to study in one go: in every lesson with timed cards (from a video or YouTube subtitles), the cards are taken in time order from 0:00 to the end and every three in a row become one card. Their translations are joined the same way as the sentence: one running line under it, like the Korean (or other) sentence above, not one line per subtitle. Lessons imported before this get their joined translations put on one line the next time the app opens (only notes made of nothing but those translation lines, each in another language than the sentence; notes you wrote yourself are left as they are). Its sentences follow each other (with no space between them in Chinese or Japanese) and so do their translations, it keeps the time of the first sentence (so its clip runs through all three), its notes are joined into one list, and it starts as a new card. The words in those notes keep their own progress in Vocabulary, which works by word and is unchanged. This happens once for the lessons you already have (when the app starts or a lesson is opened) and right away for every new import; a last one or two left over stay as they are, and a joined card is never joined again. It concerns Listening and ☰ Full; text lessons studied with 🗒️ Speaking have no times and are left alone.
- **Video cards have no answer.** A card with a video time is only its sentence and its notes: Listening studies it by ear, so it needs no translation. A translation that comes in with it (YouTube subtitles with a translation, or an answer column in a timed paste) goes into the card's notes as a line of its own, and any answer a video card still had is dropped. The card editor of a video card has no Answer box.
- **A question on its own stays where it is.** A card without a video time that has only a question (no answer) is a Lessons card, studied with 🗣 Speaking; a word added to Vocabulary without a meaning goes to Lessons as such a card by itself. Its sheet is only its text on the ruled Speaking paper, bound at its top: a white notepad sheet as wide as the Speaking paper and just as tall as its writing (no empty paper around it, the room above and below the text measured from its letters themselves, so it looks the same, also in the list's with no question parts; a long text makes it taller, so all of it shows and the page scrolls), bound at its top with binder rings (white with a dark outline, each going into a dark punched hole), with the text in the middle, bold in the paper's ink, its lines starting at one left edge, and no lesson name, question count, timer or number. A Speaking card with an answer has no heading, rule, timer, lesson name or question count either: only its question, in the lesson colour and bold, over its answer lines, with Notes beside them (under them on a phone); the writing keeps 24 px from the top and bottom edges and 48 px from the left and right (40 px on a phone). Notes are smaller than the answer, 20 px on 28 px lines (18 px on 25 px on a phone), the same as the notes in every list, each line of them 6 px from the next however they were written (the words a card holds, plain lines or a list), on every Speaking sheet and in the list; questions and answers, and a card's text, all share one line spacing on every Speaking sheet and in the list: the ruled lines' 36 px (32 px on a phone), with no heading, word or clip over them (on every Speaking sheet, with an answer or with no question, and on the task sheets); they are the words the card holds, numbered, or, when it holds none, the card's own notes as they were written. A sheet without a question is laid out as one with a question when it has Notes: its text on the left and the Notes in a column on its right, set off by a thin rule (under the text on a phone, with no rule); without Notes its text is one block in the middle of the sheet. On every Speaking sheet, with or without a question, and in the list, everything is 24 px apart top to bottom, measured from the letters themselves (the top of their accents, the bottom of their descenders, so Vietnamese accents and letters like g and y count): the rings' holes or the paper's top edge to the first line, the text to the Notes, the answer to the stroke order and dictionary, the last part to the paper's bottom edge, and one card to the next in the list, and then, as on the Speaking paper with an answer, the stroke order and the dictionary of its text; its text is bold in the paper's ink, and it keeps 18 px of room above its text, counted from the bottom of the rings' holes so the writing never touches a ring, and 24 px below and 32 px on its left and right, as do the list's sheets of Speaking cards with no question; it sits 16 px under the toolbar, as the list does; the list's with no question parts do the same (with no empty answer line under the text). A Speaking card with an answer is bound on its left with the same rings, as the notebook page is, and its Notes column is set off by a thin line in the colour of the paper's rules rather than red; it is graded Again or Good as usual. Vocabulary has no with no question flashcards. Once its answer is filled in it is sorted again and moves by itself: a Lessons card whose new answer is in another language (a translation) goes to Vocabulary, and a Vocabulary word given a meaning in its own language goes to Lessons as a Speaking card (from the editor; in Lessons also from an import that fills in a missing answer, since Vocabulary's imports only add new words); ⌘Z undoes the move. Questions, words and notes have no length limit anywhere: a long term before `:` or `=` is still a word, a long bullet still becomes a card, and any selection on a PDF can be looked up.
- **A paste stays in the lesson.** Pasting a text never makes words by itself: its sentences become the lesson's cards, and everything else, `word: meaning` lines, bullets, translations and the short headings above a list, is kept word for word in the notes of the nearest card above it (or below, when it comes first), as plain notes, not words; the preview marks those lines 📝. Words are made only by you: Shift on a selection, Add in the dictionary, ＋ in Vocabulary, or a `word: meaning` line you type into a card's notes yourself. A paste that holds only words and no sentence at all still goes to Vocabulary as before.

**Questions and answers without a video go to Vocabulary.** Lessons are for studying with YouTube: a pasted card whose answer translates its question (the two are in different languages) and that has no video time becomes a word and its meaning in Vocabulary instead of a card. When a new lesson ends up with only words, it becomes a Vocabulary lesson and opens there. Cards with a video time, and questions answered in the same language (the cards studied with Speaking), stay in the lesson, and Speaking never asks to move a card. **Every card is where it is studied, always.** The app sorts every card by its languages on its own, in every lesson, also cards made long ago and already studied: a text card whose question and answer are in different languages is a word and its meaning, so it goes to Vocabulary; a word whose meaning is in its own language (English with an English definition, say) is a question answered in its own language, so it goes to Lessons and is studied with Speaking, also a word written in a card's notes, which then leaves those notes. Each moves with its progress (a card's Speaking progress becomes the word's, and the other way round) and its notes. This happens when the app opens and every time a lesson is saved; a with no question word (no meaning) goes to Lessons as a with no question Speaking card, since Vocabulary has no with no question flashcards, while a Speaking card with no question (no answer) and the sentences of a video stay where they are, and the card or word being edited waits until its editor closes.
- Start a line with a timestamp (`1:23 question : answer`) and set the lesson's YouTube link to tie each card to a moment in the video.
- **The ＋ boxes show the card they make.** On the Add page and on a new lesson's page, where the lesson is not settled yet, the box for a single card starts as three plain boxes, Question, Answer and Notes, each with its coloured edge and nothing written in them, and takes the shape of its card as soon as you type. In a lesson's own ＋ popup it is in that lesson's shape from the start. - **Choose where a paste goes.** Above the paste box, in Lessons and Vocabulary alike, on the Add page and on a new lesson's page, **Add to** picks the lesson the pasted cards or words go into: **🆕 New lesson** comes first and is outlined in the lesson colour while it is the choice (the box is exactly as wide as the browser itself needs for the choice it shows, so it is snug and never cuts off a letter, in Safari too) (a new, still empty lesson), followed by your lessons. The parts of the add form are evenly spaced, on the Add page and in the popup, on a computer and a phone: 16 px between Add to, the ＋ bar, the paste box (and its preview) and the Column delimiter row, 8 px between the rows under it, and the same room above the first part as below the last (16 px, on the Add page and in the popup).

**How far Undo goes.** Undo and Redo keep the last 20 steps of the lesson that is open (fewer to hold in memory; nothing is written to disk, and opening another lesson or reloading starts afresh); a deletion can be undone for one minute, like Gmail's undo, after which it is final.

## Books

📚 Books sits in the logo row (in Lessons and Vocabulary alike, and in a phone's top bar), lit while the library is open; clicking it while a book is open goes back to the library. It is a **library** of the textbooks that come with the app, studied with the app's own modes and spaced repetition.

**The library.** A big banner in the colours of the language picked, with its letter (한, 中, 日, Aa) large and faint in its corner. Under it four language cards, as in the test centre: 한국어, 中文, 日本語 and English, each with its flag, how many books it has and how many are open, the items learned (✓) and an orange badge with the reviews due; the one picked is filled with its colours, a language with no books yet is dashed and pale. Each book has its own colour: a Korean book its three stage colours, any other book its cover colour.

**Learn first: the page of the book.** Each part is first a page to read, laid out like the page of the textbook, before any flashcard. At its top, ‹ Unit 5 · 요리 to go back to the unit, the unit's parts as tabs (✓ on the ones read) and **Meanings shown / Meanings hidden**. Nothing opens in the sidebar: a book's parts are kept as lessons behind the scenes (so they are saved, synced to the cloud and backed up like any lesson, and take part in Today's plan like any lesson in a language you learn) but they never show in the lesson list.

**What a part holds.** Everything in the unit is there to learn its new words; nothing is translated. Vocabulary is the unit's word lists (기본 어휘 and 새 단어) with the book's Vietnamese meanings. Every other part is the unit's Korean sentences one by one (grammar examples, model dialogues, exercise sentences, the reading texts, the culture text), each a Speaking card with no question that carries the new words in it, with their Vietnamese meaning, as its words; grammar points come as words of their own (밖에: chỉ…), so each example sentence also waits for its grammar point. As with any card, a sentence waits until its words are Done, then comes back as a whole sentence to read and say. A word is taught once in the whole book, at its first appearance. **Nothing is ever lost.** A book's words and sentences belong to the app: grade them as usual, edit them, delete them; a deleted one is not gone but goes to that part's **archive**, shown under the part as *🗄 2 in the archive · Restore*: Restore brings everything in it back, as new cards. When a book is updated (new or corrected units), your progress is kept, and what you deleted stays in the archive instead of coming back. Words are read aloud with the browser's own voice, as everywhere in the app; no sound files are added.

## Tests

**Paused for now.** Tests are switched off: 📝 is gone from the logo row and ⌘⌥X does nothing, while every test, result and setting is kept as it was. ⚙️ Settings has a **Tests** switch at its bottom (📝 and its name only); tick it and everything below comes back at once, untick it and they are put away again (leaving the test centre if it is open). The switch is kept in this browser.

When on, 📝 Tests sits in the logo row, in Lessons and in Vocabulary alike (in a phone's top bar too), counting the tests in progress and the ones due, and lit while the test centre is open. ⌘⌥X (Ctrl+Alt+X) opens it too. Tests are built for active recall: every answer is brought back from memory and typed or spoken, never picked. There is no multiple choice, no word bank, no scrambled words to put in order and no list to choose from, and each word is tested only once on a paper. A word used in one question never comes back in another part further down. When a paper needs more words than there are, it has fewer questions instead.

**Languages first.** The test centre opens on four big language cards: English, 中文, 한국어 and 日本語, each in its own colours with a large letter (Aa, 中, 한, 日), its number of words and how far its route has gone. A language opens once it has at least 3 of your Vocabulary words; until then its card is greyed and dashed. Clicking the language you are already on, or 📝 while the test centre is open, changes nothing, and the page only plays its short entrance (the banner and the route's cards rising in) when it opens or another language is picked, not on every button. Each language is its own world and never mixes with another: its papers use only its words, and everything in it is written in that language (路线, 第1步, 开始… for Chinese), except the ✦ Claude key at the top.

**The route.** Under the cards, a large banner in the language's colours names the next step of its route with a ring showing the steps passed, your words, tests, average and best score. Its buttons are:

- ▶ **Start step N** (or Resume, while that step's paper is in progress).
- 🎯 **Weak cards**: a test of the cards you missed lately and the ones with the most lapses.
- ⚡ **Quick check**: a short test of missing words and translations.
- ✎ **Build a test**: opens the builder below.

The route itself is a path of seven steps, each with its parts, number of questions, length, best score, tries, and Start, Retake and Review buttons:

1. **Recall**: dictation, missing words and translation.
2. **Use**: your own sentences, speaking and writing.
3. **Vocabulary mastery**: every vocabulary part on one paper.
4. **Understanding**: the real exam's reading, listening and word parts.
5. **Expression**: the real exam's writing and speaking parts.
6. **Mock exam**: the whole exam at standard length.
7. **Final exam**: the long version, timed like the real day.

Steps 1–3 are Vocabulary tests; steps 4–7 use the language's real exam (IELTS for English, HSK for Chinese, TOPIK for Korean, JLPT for Japanese). A step opens when the step before reaches 70 %. A step whose parts all need Claude shows ✦ Needs a Claude key and is skipped until a key is added. A finished step's score report has ▶ Next: step N to go straight on.

The rest of the language's page:

- **Waiting for you**: the tests in progress and scheduled, with Resume or Start and ✕.
- **Results**: every finished test in that language with a coloured score medal, its band and date, Review, ↻ Retake and ✕, the last five first (Show all for the rest), and a small chart of the scores.
- **Skills**: bars per skill from the last ten tests.
- **Cards to work on**: the cards you missed, with 🎯 Test these cards.

**Build your own test.** ✎ Build a test opens a builder in the language's colours. Choose the exam (Vocabulary test, or the language's exams), the words (all your words or one lesson's) and the parts, which can be switched on and off. Then choose the length (Short, Standard or Long) and, optionally, a date (Take it on). Finish with ▶ **Start now** or 📅 **Save for later**; a saved test waits as Scheduled and is marked due from that day. Each exam keeps only the question types that make you think, laid out and worded like the real one:

**The exam system.** ✕, then Delete?, removes a test. A finished test opens on its score report, a printed sheet in the same style as the paper and in its language (成绩单, SCORE REPORT…). It shows:

- the PHILLIP ACADEMY heading, the exam's name, the words and the date of the test;
- the candidate lines and the paper code;
- a ruled table with a row per part (number, part, score, out of, %) and a bold total row;
- the result (★ Excellent from 85 %, Good from 70 %, Pass from 50 %, otherwise Needs work), the time used and how many answers were fully right, beside the red score stamp.

Once the paper is marked (straight after Hand in, or after your last own mark), the page goes back to the top, to this sheet. Under the sheet, side by side, are cards that each say what they do, in the paper's language: ← Test centre (back to your route and all your results), 🎯 Practise missed cards (a short new test of the words this paper got wrong, with how many), ↻ Retake (the same exam again, with new questions) and, on a route step passed at 70 % or more, ▶ Next: step N with the step's name (the next step of your route), the one to take next filled in colour. The marked paper follows.

A test is saved as you go, every answer as you type it. Close the app, reload or come back days later, and Resume opens the paper exactly where you left it, with your typed answers and the time left. Pause on the paper does the same at once. The clock only runs while the paper is open and stops while answers are being marked.

Tests are part of the backup (💾) and come back with it. A test keeps its own copy of the words and sentences it uses (only those, once the paper is written, so tests stay small). It therefore does not depend on your lessons: deleting a lesson or a word leaves its tests, papers and scores untouched (only Retake needs the words to still be there). Test data goes away only when you delete a test, when a finished test falls beyond the 50 most recent ones, or when the browser's site data is cleared; restoring a backup brings tests back.

**The paper.** It is laid out like a real printed exam paper: plain white sheets in a serif type, headed on the left by PHILLIP ACADEMY with how many pages it has right under it (This paper has 01 page), and on the right by the exam's name in capitals, the words it uses and the time allowed; under them the candidate name and number lines on dotted rules and a boxed paper code; each part opens with one bold line (PART I – Listening, 第一部分, 一、听力…) followed by its question numbers and instructions; questions are numbered the exam's way (Câu 1., 1.), reading texts and scripts sit in a thin box, blanks are dotted lines, answers are written in blue on ruled lines, and each sheet ends with its page and paper code (Trang 1/4 - Mã đề thi 0115). A sheet holds two parts. The clock and Pause sit outside the paper, above it, and stay in view as you scroll. Recordings and questions to hear are a plain round ▶ button with two dots showing the plays left (●● then ●○): while the recording loads the button spins, and a play is only counted once the sound actually starts (it turns into ■, which stops it); if it does not start within 15 seconds, or YouTube cannot play it, nothing is counted and ▶ can be pressed again; speaking is a round ● that turns into a red ■ while listening. Once marked, the score is stamped in red in a double circle beside the examiner's handwritten-style comment (Lời phê), and each answer gets a red ✓, ✗ or score in the margin. Nothing is written twice: a fully right answer keeps only its ✓; otherwise one red line under your answer gives the right version, merged with yours: what you wrote wrong or added is struck through in grey, and what should be there, including what you left out, follows it underlined in bold (words, or characters in Chinese and Japanese), as in began ~~abduction~~ __abducting__ or 银行~~子~~, as a teacher marks a paper (capitals, spaces and punctuation are not counted as mistakes), followed by a short comment when there is one. A dictation shows its meaning under ▶ once marked, and the sentence itself only in that red line when your answer was not right. In the dark theme the sheets stay light, a little dimmer than white. Each paper speaks its exam's language, as the real one does: everything printed on it (the Candidate, Date and Marked by lines, the instructions box, Score and Examiner's comments, the part instructions and their numbering, the buttons to play a recording, speak and hand in, the end line and the examiner's comments, and the score report above a marked paper; and the test's row in the test centre and its exam card) is in Chinese on an HSK paper, Korean on TOPIK, Japanese on JLPT and English on IELTS and SAT, and a Vocabulary test is written in the language of its words; Claude's comments on each answer are in that language too. Only the Vocabulary test adds help in your own language, since translating from it is part of the test (the sentence's meaning under a missing word, the meanings of the words to use, comments in your language); a real-exam paper has none.

**Listening from your videos.** A dictation question first looks for a word whose saved sentence comes from a YouTube lesson: the ▶ then plays that very stretch of the video (from the sentence's time to the next one's), unseen, so you hear the real voice, and the sentence is what you write; only when no such sentence exists is a sentence read aloud by the browser (or, with a Claude key, a new one written and read aloud). Listening passages written by Claude are read aloud.

**Speaking.** A speaking question is answered out loud: ▶ Hear the question reads it aloud, ● Speak starts listening (the browser asks once for the microphone) and ■ Stop ends it; what you said is written on the ruled lines as you speak, and ● Speak again replaces it. The written-down words are what is marked, out of 4 (answering the question, using the given words, grammar, range of words), with spelling and punctuation ignored since they come from speech recognition. JLPT and SAT have no speaking part, as in the real exams. Where the browser cannot listen (Firefox, for one) or listening fails, the answer is typed instead.

**How a test runs.** The whole paper is written as soon as it is opened and shown at once, every part on its own sheet, as on a printed exam: answer the questions in any order, go back to any of them, and press **Hand in** at the end of the last sheet when you are done (with unanswered questions it first asks Hand in with 3 unanswered?, and a second press hands in). The words you find hardest (most lapses, never studied) are used first, each word once on the whole paper. Return moves to the next answer box (in longer answers it starts a new line). Recordings play at most twice. The time allowed adds up each question's time (for example 1 minute to listen and write, 45 seconds for a missing word, 2 minutes per reading question, 8–40 minutes for writing, by exam); the clock turns red in the last minute, and when it runs out the paper is handed in as it stands. Everything is marked after handing in; the last question of each missing-word, translation or correction part also asks you to explain why, and a wrong or missing reason halves its mark.

**Marking.** A Claude API key is a personal password for the Anthropic API, starting with sk-ant- and made at console.anthropic.com (the form links there); Anthropic charges a little for each paper written and marked, and a Claude Pro subscription does not include one. With a key (added with ✦ Add a Claude key at the top of the test centre, kept only in this browser and never offered to the browser's password or address autofill, so nothing is filled into the search; Remove key takes it out), every part is offered: Claude writes brand-new questions in the exam's style for each paper and marks each answer by its meaning against a rubric, accepting every correct alternative, not only the model answer (1 point, ½ for a small slip, 0; Writing and Speaking out of 4). Without a key, the parts that need Claude (reading and listening passages, readings, corrections, rewriting) are greyed out; the rest use your saved sentences, Listening and missing words are marked by how close your answer is, a translation, correction or rewrite almost the same as the model answer (one small slip) gets ½ by itself, and only the other open answers, which may be right in other words, are marked by you. While some are left, the bar pinned above the paper says how many (2 left to mark · Next ↓) and takes you to the next one; each mark moves the page on to the next answer to mark, and the last one brings you up to the score sheet. Under each one a MARK IT YOURSELF box, in the paper's language, shows the model answer with the differences from yours marked as in a correction (your words struck through, the model's underlined), one line on what counts, and the buttons ✓ Right, ½ Nearly and ✗ Wrong (4 to 0 for Writing and Speaking). An answer you mark right keeps only its ✓. If Claude cannot be reached during a test, the paper carries on this way.

When every word is studied, 🎧, the flag and ⇄ are hidden: only the counts, 🎉 and the word list, always open, remain until words are due again.

Words already in your vocabulary (the `question: answer` lines in notes, in any lesson) are marked by the colour of their letters only, with nothing behind them (so no edges to line up): in the same colours the highlights had, now on the letters: a clear green for a word another card also has, a clear blue for a word of this card alone (both strong enough to read at a glance, never faded, never purple; where an older line here says grey or green highlight, it means these letter colours; the yellow highlight on a flashcard's sentence stays as it was); they are marked wherever a lesson's cards are shown, in every mode and on a computer or a phone alike: the translation card, the Listening lines, the Speaking paper and the ☰ Full list, as well as the card editor (and its Notes), by the same rule everywhere, in questions and answers alike. A word shows the moment it is made: grey in the editor as soon as it is added, before you save, and grey on the card as soon as the editor closes. Green means another card, in this lesson or another one, also has the word: click (or tap) it, in the editor or right where you are studying, to see that card's sentence and note, with the lesson's name when it comes from another lesson (cards from the lesson you are in come first). The popup is as wide as its text (up to 320 px), with the same space above and below the text and on both sides; the side space only widens to make room for the ‹ › arrows when there are several matches. Inside the popup, point at the sentence, at the highlighted word in it, or at a word in the note (tap it on a phone; tap again to hide) to see its pinyin or reading, in the same small box as in the table, but light with dark text so it stands out on the dark popup. Everywhere a pinyin or reading is shown, the box holds only the reading of the Chinese characters (or of the Japanese words), never a copy of what is already on screen, and only while the pointer is on the Chinese or Japanese text itself: pointing at 去体验 in a note line such as 去体验: đi trải nghiệm shows qù tǐ yàn, while pointing at the meaning or at the empty part of a box or card shows nothing. **No hints while you guess.** In Listening and in a task, until a card is shown it gives nothing away: no pinyin or reading on pointing or tapping, no green or blue marks on its words (so no meanings to click either) and no notes; they all come with Show. Speaking is the one exception, as it has its own Hint button for asking on purpose. Once shown, the smallest thing under the pointer wins: a marked card (green, grey, or the blue card being studied) shows only its own reading, in Lessons and Vocabulary alike, on a computer and when tapped on a phone; then the sentence (on the Speaking paper); and only on the rest of the text the reading of the whole line or card. When Shift opens the box to add a word or note, any reading on screen is hidden so the box stays in view, and none appears outside the box while it is open. Meanings, Vietnamese and pinyin already written are left out (between two Chinese words they become ·, as in shū cài · qīng cài), while punctuation and a single Latin word inside a sentence stay (wǒ yòng iPhone dǎ diàn huà， hǎo ma？). Grey means only this card has it: on a computer, click it anywhere and a small box opens with its `question: answer` from the card's notes, ready to change; Return saves it (in the editor it goes into the Notes box, saved with the card), Esc closes it. On a phone a grey word is only for reading (editing it there is unreliable): tapping it, in the cards or in the editor, shows its word: meaning line in the same dark popup a green word opens, with no box to type in and no keyboard (tap the word in it for its pinyin); tapping anywhere else closes it. On a computer its box can still be edited, and pointing at the word in it shows its pinyin or reading too, just under the box and above everything else (the same in every word: meaning box, also the one for adding a word). Clicking a green word (or a grey one) only opens its popup or box; clicking anywhere else on the card does what it always does (opens the card editor or selects the card). Every word is highlighted, even inside or across another one, with the colours layered where they share characters; pointing at a word lights up all of it.

A word is looked up only where the text holds exactly that word, ignoring case, spaces and punctuation (so 送货 is found in 开车送货 and `cat.` in "a cat sat"); other forms of a word (went for go, 食べました for 食べる, 먹어요 for 먹다) are not matched, and a word is never split into its single characters to look them up one by one. Words are found by the kind of writing at their edges, with no language setting and no lists of words to skip:

- Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese, written without spaces, match anywhere.
- Korean must start a spaced chunk but may end inside it, so 학교 matches 학교에서 but not 대학교.
- Languages written with spaces (English, Vietnamese, Russian…) must match whole words, so "cat" does not match "concatenate".
- Full-width and half-width forms count as the same (ｶﾀｶﾅ and カタカナ, ＡＢＣ and ABC), katakana and hiragana count as the same (ネコ and ねこ), Vietnamese tone marks may sit on either vowel (hòa and hoà, khỏe and khoẻ, thúy and thuý), case does not matter, and line breaks count as spaces.

## Reading PDFs

A lesson can hold a PDF to read beside its video, in Lessons. The button at the start of the link box, inside it (a small page with a red PDF band), adds a PDF from your computer or phone; a PDF link pasted in the box and opened with Open is added the same way.

**Links.** The link box holds the lesson's video (YouTube, TikTok, Douyin) and opens PDFs; any other link (an article, a NotebookLM notebook, a page you read) pasted in it and opened with Open joins the lesson's **list of links** right under the box, newest last, and opens in a new tab, while the box keeps the video. The links hang together under the link bar as one cluster, as wide as the bar, 4 px apart, and sway very gently. Once there are links the bar has a hole punched near each end of its bottom edge (in the link box on the left, in Clear on the right), and every link a hole near each end of its top edge; two cords run from the bar's holes down over each link's top edge into its holes, behind the link and across the gap to the next one, ending tied in the last link's holes. With no links, nothing is punched. Each link is a slim row with a link icon, the page's title when it is known and its address: click it to open it, ✕ to remove it (Undo brings it back). Nothing put in the box is lost when something new takes its place: opening another video moves the one before it to the end of the list, and opening another PDF (a link or a file) moves the one in the reader there too, by its name (a file from this device stays in this browser, marked "PDF on this device"). Clicking a video or PDF in the list brings it back to the box or the reader, and the one there takes its place in the list.  When the browser extension sends words into a lesson, the page they were read on is added to that lesson's list by itself, once, with its title. A lesson that used to keep a page link in the box has it moved to the list. The same box, PDF reader and list are on the lesson's Vocabulary page too, in the same blue, so nothing of the lesson is missing there; in a task there is no box. Either way the lesson's video link stays where it is: the PDF comes on top of it, never instead of it. Open and Clear sit 8 px apart, the same 8 px the box keeps from Open. A ✕ at the end of the link box (as the PDF button sits at its start) empties the box and takes the video away; both only take a light colour when pointed at (the PDF button a light red, ✕ a light red too), and neither moves; Clear takes everything out at once, the video, the list of links and the PDF. Undo brings back either.

The PDF opens under the link box in a reader of its own: a plain toolbar with the file's name on the left and, on the right, the page you are on (3 / 12), zoom (− 100% +, from 50% to 200%) and ✕, which takes the PDF out of the lesson, in groups split by thin lines, every button the same 32 px; then the pages, on the same white as the rest of the app, each outlined with a soft shadow and its number under it, scrolling inside the reader (640 px tall on a computer, 480 px on a phone) and drawn only as you come near them, so long books open quickly. Words already in your lessons are highlighted in its text in the same green as in the cards; resting on one shows the same lookup as the browser extension: the word in bold, its pinyin on a line of its own for Chinese, its meaning under it when it has one (a card with only a question shows none, rather than a dash), and the lesson it is in, small and in italics (10 px of margin all round and 10 px between its parts, in the app and the browser extension alike), after the app's 字 in bold, in the same size as the name, coloured for the side it is in (green for a Vocabulary word, blue for a Lessons card), so you know where to find it. A lookup too long for the screen (a card with many notes, say) opens above or below the word, wherever there is more room, no taller than that room, and scrolls inside itself; scrolling it never moves the page or closes it. Only the words scroll: the ‹ › arrows (when the word is in several cards) and the 1 / 3 counter stay in place, whole, however far you scroll. A thin scroll bar beside the words (never over them, set in the popup's right margin so both sides stay even) and a small ⌄ button at the foot of the words (click or tap it to scroll on; it fades away once the end is reached) show there is more, and the edges fade softly as the words pass them, a short fade just like the sidebar's that grows in as you scroll rather than appearing all at once. Going to another card with ‹ › fits the popup to that card's own length and starts it at its top. A card that holds several of the words looked up (开车送货 and 送货 in one sentence, say) shows once, with each of them marked, not once per word. The ‹ › strips are slim (24 px) with their arrows in the very middle of them. A lookup keeps one measure throughout, 10 px: its margins on every side and the room between its parts (the sentence, the notes, the lesson's name and the 1 / 2 count), while the small word box that pops up on a pointed word is tighter, 8 px above and below and 10 px at the sides, with 4 px between the word, its pinyin and its meaning and 6 px before its lesson, in the app and the browser extension. Every default scroll bar in the app is the same thin bar that takes its own room beside what it scrolls, so it never covers text, and it stays the same when pointed at; the app's own scroll rails are unchanged. Wherever a lookup names the lesson a word or sentence comes from (the word popup in the cards, when the sentence is from another lesson, and the note on the reading card), the lesson's name is in italics and muted, never bold, after the app's 字 in bold, in the same size as the name, in blue (a Lessons card) or green (a Vocabulary word). A lookup is as wide as its longest line, so the words sit evenly inside it with the same room on both sides, never a wide empty strip on the right where a long sentence broke early.

**Picking words, as in the browser extension.** Select a word or phrase and the green 字 button appears beside it (Shift opens the card straight away). The card is the extension's: the word (a long phrase wraps onto more lines, under the buttons, instead of being cut off), 🔊 to hear it and a button to translate the whole sentence, the three buttons evenly spaced in the top right corner, the meaning translated for you beside a green bar (a long one wraps too) with other meanings to pick from, the sentence it came from on a light green ground with the word marked (click it to edit), a note when the word is already in one of your lessons, and Add (or Enter). The word goes into this lesson's words with its sentence, and shows in Vocabulary with it; the PDF's name is not added. On a phone, the 字 button appears once the selection has stayed still a moment. Scrolling the PDF or the page closes the card; when no translation can be had (offline, say), the meaning box simply asks you to type it.

The PDF itself stays in this browser only: the lesson (its .json file and the cloud copy) keeps just its name and link, so it stays small. A linked PDF opens again by itself on any device; a file opened from your computer opens again on this one, and on another device the reader asks you to add the file once more. Some sites do not let other apps read their PDFs; then the reader says so, and you can download the PDF and add the file instead. In the study modes the reader is hidden with the link box. A task and Vocabulary have no reader: the words you add show in Vocabulary as usual.

## Reading on the web

The **wikiPhidia** browser extension (in the [`extension`](extension) folder, with its own [instructions](extension/README.md)) lets you read any page and pick up new words as you go: select a word and press Shift (or click the green 字 next to it), check the meaning it translates for you, the pinyin and the sentence it found, and press Enter. The words go into Vocabulary by themselves as soon as wikiPhidia is open in a tab, each as a card in the lesson you chose (by default a lesson named after the article, made for you the first time and reused for the rest of its words): the word on the front and, on the back, the sentence it came from with the word marked in it, like any other card (also under the meaning in ☰ List, and read aloud with the card), then the meaning, and the article's title when the lesson has another name. A word already in any lesson is not added twice, and a toast says how many came in. The extension also marks on every page the words you already have (green) and those still waiting to be sent (grey), with their meaning on pointing, and it makes lessons from YouTube videos with one click (see **Lessons straight from YouTube**).

## Progress and sync

- **Stats** shows your daily streak, a review heatmap and progress per lesson. The heatmap shows six months at a time; ‹ and › move back and forward and ○ returns to today, and each appears only when there is somewhere to go. Click a day to list its cards just under the heatmap, grouped by lesson: on a past day the cards and words you studied (with ✓ or ✕ for how you last answered, and 字 on words), on a coming day the cards due then. Click a card to open its lesson (in Vocabulary for a word); click the day again to close the list. On a phone the heatmap opens scrolled to the latest months, each card in the day's list shows its answer on a second line, and the table of lessons keeps its names readable and scrolls sideways when it is wider than the screen.
