# wikiPhidia

Flashcards for learning Chinese and Japanese, with spaced repetition, YouTube clips, dictionaries and a vocabulary deck built from your notes.

The whole app is one file, `index.html`. There is no build step and nothing to install: open it in a browser, or host it anywhere that serves static files. On a phone it can be added to the home screen and runs full screen.

## Lessons and cards

- Paste cards into a lesson, one per line, as `question : answer` or tab-separated. The separator is detected and a preview shows what will be imported. Column delimiter offers Auto-detect, Tab and Colon ( : ), in Lessons and Vocabulary alike; only Tab also takes a time column and a notes column. **Auto-detect**, the first choice and the default on both sides, reads a whole study text in one paste, in Lessons or in Vocabulary alike: a question line just above a paragraph (ending in ?, starting with Q: or Question:, or a short heading such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes one card with the whole paragraph as its answer (an A: or Answer: in front is dropped; following paragraphs belong to the same answer), any other paragraph becomes one question-only card per sentence, each `word = meaning` or `word: meaning` line (with or without a `*`, `-`, `•` or `1.` in front) becomes a word, a line with Tab columns is read column by column as under Tab (time, question, answer, notes; it is never taken as the lesson title), and a short line just above a word list (such as `🔥 Phrasal verbs to conquer today`) is left out. Each word is filed under the card whose question or answer uses it, also in another form (`headed out` for head out, `got up` for get up, `gave up` for give up), so clicking it there shows that sentence, and the back of its flashcard shows the sentence with that form highlighted (the back always uses a sentence of the lesson that contains the word, even if the word was filed under another card); a word no sentence uses goes to the lesson's own word list. A short first line without sentence punctuation (such as `Day 1 — A Busy Morning`) becomes the lesson title when the lesson is empty, and stays the question of its card when a paragraph follows it. A dialogue (lines such as `Patient: …` and `Dentist: …`, where the same speakers come back) is kept as it is under Auto-detect: each line is one card with its speaker, never split at the colon, and a line ending in ? is not taken as the question of the line under it. From Auto-detect, pasted cards or words still switch to Tab or colon by themselves when more than half the lines use it and no line holds several sentences (a colon after a finished sentence does not count, nor do Q:, A:, Question: and Answer: labels, so a paste of short Q: and A: lines stays questions and answers); from any other choice, Auto-detect is picked when no line has a Tab or colon. When a paste into a text lesson (from Lessons or Vocabulary) has no word list, its sentences become Vocabulary words themselves: each sentence of a long answer, or of a paragraph, is translated (a card with a one-sentence answer stays a Speaking card only), Vietnamese into English and anything else into Vietnamese; the preview counts them as 🔤 from sentences and the status line says how many were added. English or Vietnamese under Translate to splits sentences the same way and fills in a translation as the answer. Bullets in front of a line are dropped with every delimiter.
- **Questions and answers without a video go to Vocabulary.** Lessons are for studying with YouTube: a pasted card whose answer translates its question (the two are in different languages) and that has no video time becomes a word and its meaning in Vocabulary instead of a card, also when made with Translate to. When a new lesson ends up with only words, it becomes a Vocabulary lesson and opens there. Cards with a video time, and questions answered in the same language (the cards studied with Speaking), stay in the lesson, and Speaking never asks to move a card.
- Start a line with a timestamp (`1:23 question : answer`) and set the lesson's YouTube link to tie each card to a moment in the video.
- **New things go on top.** Every way of adding, one at a time or pasted in bulk, puts the new cards at the top of the lesson and the new words at the top of the word list (a pasted batch keeps its own order). The ＋ on the Add cards page opens ☰ Full with the new card at the top, and the ＋ on Add vocabularies opens ☰ List with the new word at the top.
- Each card has rich-text notes. Lines written as `word: meaning` become words in the vocabulary deck. A word made from the card's own sentence (select part of the question, or of the answer in Speaking, and press Shift, in the editor or on the Speaking paper) first opens a small `word: meaning` box, everywhere alike; nothing is added until you fill in the meaning and press Return (Esc closes it, so a stray Shift adds nothing). The word then goes into the notes (in the editor, into its Notes box, saved with the card) in the order the words come in the sentence: make the fourth word first and then the first, and the first goes above the fourth. Words that are not in the sentence (typed or pasted yourself) are left where they are, and a new word not in the sentence goes at the end. A word can be any length and may contain colons, so a whole sentence such as `Second: don't brush too hard.` moves to Vocabulary as it is, with its meaning.
- Questions, answers and notes keep only basic formatting: bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, line breaks and bullet or numbered lists. Anything else pasted in (font sizes, colours, fonts, headings, links, images) is dropped, so a paste from a web page, Word or Google Docs cannot change how a card looks.
- Lessons can be saved as `.json` files and opened again, one at a time or all at once.

## Studying

| Mode | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| ☰ Full | The whole lesson as a list, for browsing and editing. No spaced repetition. Above the list it shows the 🆕 ⏳ ✅ (New, Due, Done) counts, like the study modes, each count lit up when it is not zero (hidden once everything is studied). The counts, here and in every mode on both sides, are only for looking at: clicking them does nothing, they cannot be selected as text (nor can the New, Due and Done totals and column heads in Statistics), and pointing at one turns the pointer into a question mark and says what it counts (New — never studied yet, Due — ready to review now, Done — 5 Good in a row). In a lesson made from subtitles only (no answers), the counts are those of its Listening cards, here, in the lesson list, in Statistics and in the calendar alike. A round ＋ button in the lesson colour floats at the bottom right of the list, over the cards as you scroll and just under the last one at the end of the list (on a phone, above the bottom bar, and higher while a card is selected): click it to add a card at the top of the list. It is always there in ☰ Full, and the list itself has no bar or Question / Answer header row. Vocabulary's ☰ List works the same way, its ＋ adding a word (All vocabularies has no ＋). Both lists are built alike: rows of the same height and type size, the text evenly spaced above and below, alternate rows lightly tinted in the lesson colour instead of grey (pale blue in Lessons, pale green in Vocabulary), lines between rows running edge to edge, and pointing at a row shades the whole row in the light lesson colour, the same single colour a selected row has (these two lists are the only places that shade on pointing). A new, empty lesson shows the add form with its own ＋, and a lesson whose last card is moved or deleted opens Add cards, with the lesson picked, as Vocabulary opens Add vocabularies when its last word goes. ☰ Full works exactly like Vocabulary's ☰ List: on a computer, click a card (its question or its answer) to open it in the editor, and point at it and press C to copy its question or Delete to delete it; on a phone, tap a card once to select it and show the copy and ✕ bar, and tap it twice in quick succession to open the editor. Pointing at a card in ☰ Full of a video lesson shades the whole row, the same as pointing at a word in ☰ List, and a click anywhere on the row (or on a word's row) opens the editor; the text lesson's notebook page and every study mode shade nothing when you point at them. In Listening, pointing at the question, the answer or a line of notes shows that one's pinyin or reading, with no shading, and a click on the text opens the editor.  In a text lesson the list is a notebook page: white ruled paper with a red margin (on a phone there is no margin line, and the text sits a little further in from both edges), each question as a bold heading in the lesson colour with its answer written on the lines below, and the editor puts the short question on one line above a large answer box, the same size and not bold, without a dictionary. No table has a column of ✎, ✕ or 字 buttons, on a computer or a phone, in Lessons or in Vocabulary: point at a card (or select it on a phone) and press E to edit it, Delete to delete it or, in a text lesson, V to move it to Vocabulary, flying off as the logo (on a phone the bar of copy, ✕ and 字 described below does the same): the question becomes a word, the answer its meaning, and words filed under the card go along; if the sentence is already a word, the card just leaves the lesson. Undo brings it back. In every editor a coloured line starts each box: blue for the question, green for the answer, grey for the notes, and the answer box fits its text. Only the notes box is named (Notes); the question and answer boxes carry no Question or Answer label, their colour line telling them apart. Every editor writes the question in plain (not bold) type, the same size as the answer; outside the editors, the notebook and Speaking paper questions and the words in Vocabulary's ☰ List stay bold. |
| Group (translation practice) | One due card at a time, to translate. Shown in video lessons. There is no video above it: the page starts with the mode buttons, as in the other modes, and the clip still plays (unseen) through the YouTube button beside the speed, the time badge or 1 – 3. It looks like a translator app: one white card with a language bar across the top (🇨🇳 中文 ⇄ 🇻🇳 Vietnamese, each language worked out from the text, or the lesson's study language for the question), and under it two panels side by side, the side you read on the left and the side you translate into on the right in a panel with the app's light colour for that side (light green for an answer, light blue for a question, as on the flashcards and the Good button); the card is the same size before and after it is shown, since the hidden side keeps the room its text and notes will take, divided by a thin line (on a phone the two panels sit one above the other); each language name centred over its own panel; the side not shown yet holds three round dots, as the old chat bubbles did; once the card is shown, its notes sit under the translation. Nothing else sits under the card: the stroke order and the dictionary live in the card editor, so the page stays as short as the card and never jumps when a card goes. It works both ways, switched with the round ⇄ in the middle of the language bar or F (switching starts the card over as if it had just come up: whatever is playing stops and both sides are hidden again as the new direction needs), remembered for next time, as ⇄ does for Vocabulary's flashcards: reading (the question on the left, translate it into the answer) or translating (the answer on the left, translate it back into the question); the language bar swaps with it, and ⇄ lights up while translating. The text is 28 px on 40 px lines (24 px on 35 px on a phone), a little roomier than a flashcard so the time badge never touches the line below. The time badge sits in front of the question and fills with the lesson colour while the clip plays. Clicking it while it is lit stops the sound, whatever started it (the badge, Space or the sound button). All of it is in plain type (weight 400), on a computer and on a phone alike. Both sides of a flashcard are 28 px (24 px on a phone); notes shown outside the editor are always about 85 % of the main text they sit with, never small, rounded to the app's whole-pixel sizes (24 px on a flashcard with 28 px text, 20 px on a phone with 24 px; 18 px on the Speaking paper with 20 px text); every text, main text and notes alike, is spaced the same way, its line about one and a quarter times its size, in whole pixels (main text: 15 px on 25 px lines in the lists, 28 px on 40 px in Group and Listening (24 px on 35 px on a phone), 20 px on 25 px for the notebook's questions and the Speaking question, 18 px on 22 px on a phone, 28 px on 35 px on a flashcard and 24 px on 30 px on a phone; notes: 24 px on 30 px, 20 px on 25 px, and 18 px on roomier 25 px lines on the notebook page and the Speaking paper), only the ruled answer lines of the notebook and the Speaking paper keep the paper's 36 px rule; labels and controls use fixed whole-pixel lines too (14 px buttons on 16 px lines, so they keep their 34 px height on every browser; 15 px labels such as the translation card's language names and the Listening time on 20 px; the Speaking paper's heading and clock on 28 px; the sidebar's All vocabularies like its LESSONS heading, 26 px, with its counts on 18 px), never the browser's own "normal" spacing; the rows of ☰ Full and of Vocabulary's ☰ List stay the same height; a question and its answer are always the same size, in every mode (the Speaking paper 20 px, on a computer and on a phone alike; the ☰ Full notebook 20 px), with whole-pixel line heights everywhere. The phone's browser is told not to enlarge text by itself. Click the notes (tap them on a phone) to edit them right there, like the answer (also tapped on a phone): Return saves, Shift + Return starts a new line, Esc cancels, and undo brings the old notes back. The sound works as before (▶ plays the card, 1 loops it). Editing (click the shown card, two quick taps on a phone, or E) opens exactly the same editor popup as Listening, next to where you clicked (see **The editor popup opens where you click** below): Question, Answer and Notes, Save, then the sentence's stroke order (a character can be tapped or clicked to draw it again) and its dictionary at full size; it scrolls when it does not fit, a click outside or Save keeps the changes, and Esc throws them away. The time badge in front of the question: clicking it loops that sentence, or stops it when it is the one looping, and it lights up while the sentence plays. In the table too, a card without video has 🔊 in front of its question, as in Vocabulary's Group, to read it aloud over and over (click again to stop). |
| Listening | Laid out like YouTube, video and sentence in one single white frame (one border, one shadow, rounded only at its outer corners): on a computer the video on the left and, on the right, the sentence being studied, its side exactly as tall as the video (its height worked out in whole pixels); on a phone the video on top and the sentence under it, the frame filling the screen down to the bars at the bottom (see **One card, the whole height** below). There is no list of the other sentences: one sentence at a time, the next one coming up once it is graded (New, Due and Done as everywhere). Everything stays pinned: on a phone the page itself never scrolls in Listening, and the video sits in the layout itself (it never trails behind or jumps when the phone's browser moves the page). The sentence is set as large as the translation card in Group, with the same room around it: 28 px on 40 px lines with 25 px above and below and 30 px at the sides (24 px on 35 px lines and 20 px all round on a phone), in the same plain (not bold) type. There is no frame or box: the video floats on the page with 20 px corners and a soft shadow in the lesson colour, over a gentle glow of the lesson colour that fades out well before the page's edges (like YouTube's ambient light), and beside it (under it on a phone) the sentence stands straight on the page, left-aligned, 25 px from the video; on a computer, where it sits beside the video, its whole block (question, answer and notes) is centred between the video's top and bottom edges, and before it is shown the question's line already sits where it will be once everything appears (the room of the answer and notes is kept, invisible); on a phone it starts 20 px under the video and 20 px from both sides. The lines are spaced evenly: the visible gap between the bottom of one line's letters and the top of the next (question, answer, the notes' chips) is the same as the gap to the video, 25 px on a computer and 20 px on a phone: the time badge in front of the question, then the answer in green, then the card's notes as small rounded chips side by side (15 px on 20 px lines, white with a thin border), one chip per word. Until it is shown there is only the question's line: its time badge and Group's three big dots (···), exactly where the question will be once shown, and the answer and notes simply appear under it; The frame never grows: if the sentence and its notes do not fit, the text scrolls inside it (mouse wheel, trackpad or a swipe of the finger), and the page does not. Click or tap the time badge to play the clip over and over (again to stop). Until the sentence being studied is shown, the video keeps playing but heavily blurred under a light dark veil, with a white sound wave and the clip's time over it, so you still see its colours and movement while its burnt-in subtitles can never be read; the bars sway slowly and gently only while the clip is playing and stand still when it is not, and clicking or tapping the video plays or stops the sentence being studied, before and after it is shown, exactly like its time badge (the video itself never takes the click, so YouTube's own pause and controls never get in the way). Once the sentence is shown the cover fades away quickly and the video comes into focus in under half a second, shrinking back to its size around its own centre (it never slides to a corner), and plays sharp in the frame. That is the only moment it animates, and switching to Listening from another mode never animates anything: when the video finishes loading, when the next sentence covers it again or when anything else changes, the cover simply is or is not there (a lesson with no video keeps the cover). The sound follows the same rules as before: the clip plays by itself when a card comes up; Space shows the card and from then on its clip plays over and over (also when Space is pressed while the clip is still playing by itself: it finishes that pass and then keeps looping), or it is read aloud over and over when it has none, until you stop it with Space or the sound button. Editing (E, or two quick taps on a phone) opens the card editor as a popup next to where you clicked, the sentence staying where it is behind it: Question (blue), Answer (green) and Notes (grey), Save, then the sentence's stroke order and its dictionary at its full size, spaced exactly like the card editor in ☰ Full (10 px between parts, 10 px top and bottom, 12 px each side); when it all does not fit, the popup scrolls (the dictionary never shrinks, and is cut off cleanly at the popup's edge while you scroll). Save or a click outside keeps the changes, Esc throws them away, and while it is open the study keys do nothing. Swiping on a phone flies the whole thing, video and sentence together, off like one card with 20 px corners, its outline wrapping exactly the video and the text that is showing with its usual margin (only the badge and dots before the sentence is shown, down to the last chip of notes after), never the empty space below; underneath is the next sentence as it will come up, the video turned to the blurred veil and sound wave with the next sentence's time, and its time badge and dots, shown at once. Grading grades the card, Delete deletes it, and the copy button copies it. On a phone the copy and ✕ bar acts on the card showing. Shown when the lesson has video cards. |
| 🗒️ Speaking | Laid out as an exam paper, one question at a time, for speaking practice of any kind: a white ruled sheet headed with the lesson's name, Question 2 of 5 and a clock that runs while you answer out loud; the heading stays in the same place when the answer is shown, so nothing jumps. The question sits just above the answer's ruled lines (6 px), and the lines are 36 px apart, roomy rather than cramped, exactly as in the ☰ Full notebook. The question is numbered, in the lesson colour; the ruled lines below it show ···, and the answer comes in two steps, the same with a click (or tap) on the paper, Space, Return or the phone's button (which reads Hint, then Show): the first writes a hint on those lines, the meanings of the lesson's words in the answer, in the order they come, without the words themselves, in the paper's normal ink, numbered with the numbers lined up on the left (the same in Notes); the second writes the answer there. A question without notes skips the hint and shows the answer at once. The clock is not clickable. Before the answer is shown the hint is the only help. Pointing at anything Chinese or Japanese shows its pinyin or reading, everywhere and in every kind of lesson (on the Speaking paper, whose answers are long, pointing at or tapping the question or the answer shows only the sentence under the pointer or finger, just under that sentence, and moving to the next sentence shows the next one): questions and answers in every mode, each line of notes (the word before its colon), the Speaking paper and its Notes, the notebook page, the flashcards, the word lists, the popups and every box of the card editor. The answer then comes on the left, with the lesson's words marked green or grey as everywhere else (no highlighter pen), and a numbered Notes list of those words and meanings on the right, divided from it by a red line; Notes takes only as much width as its lines need (up to a little under half the paper), so short notes leave the answer more room and long ones are not broken over two lines without need, and each note sits a little apart from the next (on a phone, Notes comes under the answer); the clock stops. As in Listening, once the answer is shown it stays until you grade: Space, a click on the paper and the phone's button (greyed out) do nothing more. ← or → grades, as everywhere. The paper takes the normal dark background in the dark theme. Everything the question does elsewhere, the answer does here: once the answer is shown, the foot of the paper, across its whole width under the answer and Notes, shows the stroke order of its characters and the dictionary for it (there is no separate panel; the paper's sections sit 32 px apart and its dictionary is 360 px tall, like the others, the dictionary moves with the page as you scroll, and the dictionaries of the current and next questions load while the question is still showing). Pointing at the answer keeps its ruled lines and shows a hand, since clicking it opens the card editor, as in Listening, and selecting part of the answer on the paper and pressing Shift adds it as a word. On a computer there are no buttons for the card: E edits it, Delete deletes it and V moves it to Vocabulary; on a phone copy, ✕ and 字 are in the bar above the bottom buttons, and two quick taps open the editor. There is no sound button, and Space does nothing once the answer is shown. In a text lesson the card editor does the same with the answer box: it highlights the words, Shift adds a selected part as a word, and the dictionary under it translates the answer. Shown in lessons without a video; it uses the same spaced repetition as Group. |

In a video lesson the video sits in a player bar as wide as the lesson, fully transparent, also while it is pinned over the cards: the video plays in the small picture on the left, beside its title and a progress bar you can drag with the time and length. Click the picture and the video grows out of it, with a genie effect, into a large player as wide as the lesson, shown in full; in ☰ Full the video is always this large player, with nothing to grow or shrink, in Translation practice it starts as the small picture, and Listening shows no video at all (its player strip is the sound button, and the video plays out of sight); the button in its corner shrinks it back into the bar. The video never shows YouTube's own buttons or menus (no settings, quality or speed sheet popping up over it, small or large), only the picture: the large player has the same title and draggable progress bar with the time and length under it, 16 px below, and the speed beside the mode buttons sets the speed. The bar stays pinned at the top as you scroll; the large player scrolls away with the page while the mode buttons stay pinned, and making it large (with the picture or Y) scrolls the page back up to it. ▶ and the speed beside the mode buttons work as before. The mode buttons have no tooltips.

**Dictionaries are ready before you open them.** In every mode, the dictionaries of the cards on screen, and of the next ones, load out of sight while you study, so a card's editor shows its dictionary at once instead of a blank box. The editor uses exactly the dictionary page that was loaded for the card.

**On a phone,** as on a computer, tables have only the question and answer columns, so they get the whole width. Every embedded page (each dictionary, in every mode, and the video) works at the first touch, with no selecting first and no outline: swiping over a dictionary scrolls the dictionary's own page, and once it reaches the end the whole page carries on scrolling. In a Group, as in Listening, the first tap on a card shows the group; once it is shown, tap a card to select it. Tapping the same card twice in quick succession opens it for editing, in Group, Listening, Speaking (anywhere on the paper, before or after the answer is shown) and ☰ Full, on a word in Vocabulary's ☰ List, and on a flashcard once it is turned. The card editor opens right where the card was, at the same height on the screen, without scrolling, 16 px under the counts when it is the first thing below them (as in Listening); only in Group are the question and answer boxes side by side, everywhere else (☰ Full, Listening, Speaking, flashcards, a text lesson's notebook, Vocabulary) they are one above the other; in Listening only the line being edited turns into the editor and the other lines stay in place. Tap a row to select it (it turns the light lesson colour with a lesson-coloured bar down its left edge, blue in Lessons and green in the Vocabulary ☰ List, on plain and tinted rows alike; in ☰ Full the row playing is light green): a bar of copy (two overlapping pages), ✕ and 字, tinted in the lesson colour like the mode buttons, (字 only where it is offered on a computer) appears, centred, just above the bottom bar of undo, redo and the study buttons, and does exactly what C and Delete do on a computer. In Group and Listening it is always there and acts on the card showing. The study modes (Group, Listening, Speaking) no longer select anything when you tap, since they show one card at a time: tapping a part of the shown card (its question, its answer, or one line of its notes) shows that part's pinyin or reading, tapping it again hides it, and two quick taps open the editor; only ☰ Full and ☰ List, which hold many cards, still select a row on a tap. On a flashcard and the Speaking paper it is always there and acts on the card showing. In ☰ Full and ☰ List it shows only once a card or word is tapped, and acts on that one. The bar has no ✎ anywhere: tapping twice opens the editor, the flashcard (once turned) and the Speaking paper included. Copy, on the left, copies as C does: the questions only (the question and its answer on the Speaking paper), as an A. B. C. list when there are several (a single one is copied as it is); a word on its own (without its meaning) in Vocabulary; in ☰ Full the question of the card you tapped. On a flashcard and the Speaking paper this bar stands in for buttons in the toolbar, so nothing sits at the top.

A lesson is either a video lesson (it has a YouTube, TikTok or Douyin link, or cards with video times), studied with Group and Listening, or a text lesson, studied with Speaking; adding or removing the link switches between them, also in the middle of a session. Group and Listening share one queue: a card studied in one mode leaves the others until it is due again.

**The card you are working on plays.** In Group and Listening, anything you do with a card, however small (tapping or clicking it, a green or grey word in it, its editor, or the dictionary in its editor), plays that card's clip over and over (or reads it aloud when it has no clip), as the dictionary panels used to. If you stop the sound while on that card, it stays quiet until you play it again.

**Showing the back lets the sound finish.** In the modes where the front and the back play the same sound (Listening, Translation practice and Vocabulary's flashcards), if a card's clip or reading is playing on the front when you show the back (Space, Return, a tap or the phone's button), it is not cut off or started over: it plays to the end of the round it is on, and only then does the back's usual sound take over (in Listening the card's clip over and over, in Translation practice the card played in turn). When what is playing is already the back's sound (the card's clip looping, the group playing, or a flashcard word's sound), it simply carries on. Stopping the sound, grading or moving on in the meantime cancels the hand-over. Speaking has no sound.

**Sound.** The YouTube button (or Space, once the cards are shown) plays the cards' video clips, at the speed beside it. Cards without a clip are read aloud in their turn, so clips and reading alternate; in a lesson without a video the button becomes 🔊 and reads the questions aloud. The speed sits beside the button whether it plays clips or reads aloud, in Lessons and in Vocabulary; reading aloud has its own speed, 1x to start, and clips theirs, 1.25x to start, and the box shows the one in use. While sound plays, the button shows a pause mark (the YouTube mark with ⏸ bars for clips, ⏸ for reading) and is filled with the mode's colour; press it again to stop. Sound also stops at once, mid-word, when you grade or move on, switch mode, lesson or view, or switch between Lessons and Vocabulary. Showing a group, or showing or flipping a word in Vocabulary, plays it automatically.

**Dictionaries are the same size everywhere.** Every dictionary (under the Speaking paper, in the editor popup of Group and Listening and in the card editor) is the full width of what holds it, with even room on both sides, and the same height: 360 px on a computer; on a phone 45 % of the screen's height (between 240 and 480 px, in whole even pixels), so it suits the device and changes with it.

**One card, the whole height.** On a phone, Listening's frame is always the same size: its top stays 16 px under the counts and its bottom 16 px above the copy ✕ bar (or above the bottom bar when that bar is not there), so nothing moves up or down from one sentence to the next. The size is checked on every frame the screen draws, so it is right straight away, also just after the page loads or when the phone's browser bars come and go; a long sentence scrolls inside its panel and the page itself does not scroll at all.

**Click to show, then click to edit.** In every study mode, clicking or tapping a card that is not shown yet shows it (the same as Space); only a card that is already shown opens its editor when clicked (two quick taps on a phone). The time badge always plays its clip.

**Time badges never touch the line below.** The time badge in front of a question keeps its normal size everywhere; instead the lines that start with one are spaced wider (25 px in the ☰ Full list, 40 px in Group and Listening, see the sizes above), so it stays clear of a highlighted word on the next line in every mode, on a computer and on a phone.

**The editor popup opens where you click.** In Group and Listening the editor is a popup about a phone's width (440 px, never wider than the screen less 16 px on each side) and at most 600 px or 70 % of the screen tall, scrolling inside when its stroke order and dictionary need more. It appears at once, without any animation, with a corner at the spot you clicked or tapped: click on the left half of the screen and it opens to the right, on the right half to the left, on the top half downwards and on the bottom half upwards, always kept 16 px inside the screen (opened with E, it sits at the card's question). The page behind it is not dimmed, and it stays in place when its stroke order or dictionary arrives.

**The card editor is spaced the same everywhere.** In ☰ Full, Speaking and the editor popup of Group and Listening the editor keeps 10 px between Question, Answer, Notes, Save and the dictionary, and 10 px top and bottom and 12 px each side inside its box (only the ☰ Full notebook keeps its wider left margin for the red line). **Scheduling.** *Again* brings a card back in 10 minutes. Each *Good* counts towards a streak; until the fifth Good in a row the card returns the next day. After five it is **done**, and the gaps grow to 1, 3 and then 7 days.

A word shows the moment it is made: grey in the editor as soon as it is added, before you save, and grey on the card as soon as the editor closes. Green means another card, in this lesson or another one, also has the word: click (or tap) it, in the editor or right where you are studying, to see that card's sentence and note, with the lesson's name when it comes from another lesson (cards from the lesson you are in come first). The popup is as wide as its text (up to 320 px), with the same space above and below the text and on both sides; the side space only widens to make room for the ‹ › arrows when there are several matches. Inside the popup, point at the sentence, at the highlighted word in it, or at a word in the note (tap it on a phone; tap again to hide) to see its pinyin or reading, in the same small box as in the table, but light with dark text so it stands out on the dark popup. Grey means only this card has it: on a computer, click it anywhere and a small box opens with its `word: meaning` from the card's notes, ready to change; Return saves it (in the editor it goes into the Notes box, saved with the card), Esc closes it. On a phone a grey word is only for reading (editing it there is unreliable): tapping it, in the cards or in the editor, shows its word: meaning line in the same dark popup a green word opens, with no box to type in and no keyboard (tap the word in it for its pinyin); tapping anywhere else closes it. On a computer its box can still be edited, and pointing at the word in it shows its pinyin or reading too, just under the box and above everything else (the same in every word: meaning box, also the one for adding a word). Clicking a green word (or a grey one) only opens its popup or box; clicking anywhere else on the card does what it always does (opens the card editor or selects the card). Every word is highlighted, even inside or across another one, with the colours layered where they share characters; pointing at a word lights up all of it.

Words are found by the kind of writing at their edges, with no language setting and no lists of words to skip:

- Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese, written without spaces, match anywhere.
- Korean must start a spaced chunk but may end inside it, so 학교 matches 학교에서 but not 대학교.
- Languages written with spaces (English, Vietnamese, Russian…) must match whole words, so "cat" does not match "concatenate".
- Japanese verbs and adjectives also match in any form: 食べる highlights 食べました and 食べない. A word that covers part of a kanji compound highlights the whole compound, so its furigana stays in one piece.
- English words also match their other forms: -s, -es, -ies, -ed, -ied and -ing (cats, watches, studied, stopped, running, making), common irregular verbs, plurals and comparisons (went for go, children for child, better for good), and each word of a phrase (gave up for give up). In a phrase, a, an and the stand for each other and may be left out (a travel pass highlights the travel pass and travel passes, issue a travel pass highlights issued travel passes), sb, sth, someone and something stand for one to three words (give sb a hand highlights gave my brother a hand), one's, your, his… stand for any of them (make up one's mind highlights made up her mind), oneself for myself, himself…, and a hyphen counts as a space (well-known and well known). A highlight means the text holds a real form of the word, and you decide whether the meaning fits (left is a form of leave even in turn left). Look-alikes that are not forms are never matched: -er, -est and -ly are left out (corner, forest, only), and evening, thing or seed are not read as forms of even, the or see.
- Korean verbs and adjectives written with -다 match their usual conjugations: 먹다 highlights 먹어요 and 먹었어요, 가다 highlights 가요, 갔어요 and 갑니다, 보다 highlights 봐요, and 공부하다 highlights 공부해요 and 공부했어요. The common irregular verbs match too: 덥다 → 더워요, 돕다 → 도와요, 듣다 → 들어요, 낫다 → 나아요, 모르다 → 몰라요, 그렇다 → 그래요, 바쁘다 → 바빠요. A form can belong to more than one word (가요 is also a noun, 들어요 also comes from 들다); it is highlighted for each, and you decide which fits.
- Full-width and half-width forms count as the same (ｶﾀｶﾅ and カタカナ, ＡＢＣ and ABC), katakana and hiragana count as the same (ネコ and ねこ), Vietnamese tone marks may sit on either vowel (hòa and hoà, khỏe and khoẻ, thúy and thuý), case does not matter, and line breaks count as spaces.

## Progress and sync

- **Stats** shows your daily streak, a review heatmap and progress per lesson. The heatmap shows six months at a time; ‹ and › move back and forward and ○ returns to today, and each appears only when there is somewhere to go. Click a day to list its cards just under the heatmap, grouped by lesson: on a past day the cards and words you studied (with ✓ or ✕ for how you last answered, and 字 on words), on a coming day the cards due then. Click a card to open its lesson (in Vocabulary for a word); click the day again to close the list. On a phone the heatmap opens scrolled to the latest months, each card in the day's list shows its answer on a second line, and the table of lessons keeps its names readable and scrolls sideways when it is wider than the screen.
- Everything is stored in the browser; no account is needed.
- Sign in with Google or email to sync lessons and reviews across devices through Firebase.
- Light and dark themes.
